# Texas Early Voting Rosters: County and State Data Review

**Election:** November 3, 2026 General Election\
**Data through:** Monday, October 26, 2026 (eighth day of in-person early voting)\
**Prepared:** October 27, 2026 (updates the reviews of October 20-26; county files as posted by 3:09 PM October 27, with Guadalupe's October 26 roster and Wichita's latest files by 9:39 PM; state files as posted by 11:16 PM October 27)\
**Scope:** the 50 most populous Texas counties and the Texas Secretary of State (SOS) roster portal

## 1. Summary

Texas Election Code §87.121 requires each county to post its early voting rosters (in-person and by mail) daily and to submit them for statewide elections to the Secretary of State, which republishes them. This update compares the county and state lists voter by voter (by Voter Unique Identifier, VUID) for the first eight days of in-person voting, October 19-26, in the 50 most populous counties.

Key findings:

1. **The two sources agree closely, but not completely, in every county.** Over every day for which both are complete, the county's and the state's lists of early in-person voters agree on 99.0% or more of voters in 32 of the 33 counties with dated county rosters, and on 98.7% in Guadalupe, compared for the first time. In none of them do the two lists match exactly: every county has at least one voter on one list and not the other. (In this review a 100% figure is shown only when no voter is on one list and not the other.) Seven voters are an in-person voter in one source and a mail voter in the other (Hunt 5, Hidalgo 1, Cameron 1).
2. **October 26 is in for every county but Webb, and Harris's file is partial.** Every one of the 50 counties has a state in-person file for each of October 19-25, all complete against the counties' own lists. For October 26, Webb has no state file and Harris's holds 42,430 of the county's 79,474 voters; every other county's October 26 file holds at least 90% of the county's count.
3. **The state keeps revising earlier days.** Since the evening of October 26, 184 county-day counts for October 14-25 changed in the state's files: 133 mail counts (net +1,094 voters, 65 of them downward) and 51 in-person counts (net +30). The state moves mail ballots between days: Brazoria's October 20 mail rose from 402 to 1,190, while Kaufman's October 20 fell from 410 to 68 as its October 15-18 files appeared for the first time (484 voters). The state also filled in its October 26 files during the day: Collin's grew from 15,259 to 30,506 records between the afternoon and the evening downloads, Bexar's from 40,435 to 44,969.
4. **Nine voters are recorded as voting in two counties.** New since the last review, now that Guadalupe's rosters are compared: one voter on Smith's in-person roster for October 19 and on Guadalupe's for October 21 (same name), whom the state lists only in Smith's October 19 file. The other eight are unchanged (§6.8).
5. **Three more voters have both a mail ballot and an in-person vote.** In Dallas, Denton and El Paso, a voter whose mail ballot the county lists as received October 20-24 voted in person on October 26. Eighteen voters are now listed both ways in the same county (§6.9).
6. **The turnout table still overstates Travis, Ellis, Hays, Coryell and Henderson.** For October 23 the portal's table shows 50,268 Travis in-person voters, against 40,484 in the state's own Travis file and 40,453 in Travis County's roster. For October 25 it shows 513 Henderson voters, against 441 in both the state's file and the county's list.
7. **Guadalupe's in-person rosters are now compared.** On October 27 Guadalupe re-posted its in-person rosters for October 19-23 under new names and added October 24-26; Election Explorer's roster loader now reads them. They agree with the state's files on 98.7% of voters (33,865 on both, 440 only in Guadalupe's lists, 73 only in the state's, 17 of them voters of other counties). The lists also hold 158 rows without a VUID and 59 seven- or eight-digit county numbers.
8. **Angelina posted October 23-26 at once, with repeated rows.** Its October 25 file is cumulative (October 19-25), its October 24 file holds only 237 of that day's voters, and its new files list 132 voters two or three times on the same day.
9. **Two large counties' mail lists trail the state's.** The state's October 26 mail files hold 927 Dallas voters and 710 Collin voters who are not on the counties' current lists; Collin's list "through October 26" ends with ballots received October 25.
10. **Williamson's mail precincts are still wrong.** Its October 26 file gives a precinct different from the state's for 4,689 mail voters (and one in-person voter).
11. **Bowie's October 20, 21 and 23 reports disagree with the state** on the precinct of 4,718 voters; its October 22 and 24-26 reports match.
12. **The state's county files contain voters of other counties.** Across October 14-26, 1,778 voters (1,753 in-person, 25 mail) appear in an SOS county file while being on a different county's own roster, with that county's precinct.
13. **The state's files continue to name voters that Tarrant County redacts,** while Tarrant's own cumulative in-person list holds fewer redacted records each day: 104 on October 20, 47 on October 27.
14. **Records that warrant review by the counties** (none is proof of wrongdoing; most have clerical explanations):
    - 18 voters listed both as a returned mail ballot and as an early in-person voter in the same county (El Paso 6, Dallas 5, Tarrant 2, Bastrop, Denton, Grayson, Jefferson and Travis 1 each);
    - 39 VUIDs that name entirely different people in two records, 9 of them on Bell's roster;
    - 163 same-day duplicate check-ins in 12 counties (132 of them in Angelina), and 31 mail ballots listed as received on two days (11 in Bexar);
    - precinct differences besides Williamson and Bowie: Harris 1,274, Tarrant 188, Galveston 163, El Paso 103, Bexar 68.

## 2. Scope and method

**Counties (by 2025 Census population):**

- Ranks 1-20: Harris, Dallas, Tarrant, Bexar, Travis, Collin, Denton, Fort Bend, Hidalgo, El Paso, Montgomery, Williamson, Cameron, Brazoria, Bell, Galveston, Nueces, Lubbock, Hays, Webb.
- Ranks 21-50: McLennan, Jefferson, Smith, Brazos, Ellis, Johnson, Kaufman, Comal, Guadalupe, Midland, Parker, Ector, Grayson, Randall, Taylor, Rockwall, Wichita, Gregg, Hunt, Liberty, Tom Green, Bastrop, Potter, Bowie, Victoria, Henderson, Angelina, Orange, Coryell, Walker.

**Sources:**

- each county's own roster files, downloaded daily October 20-27. 34 counties post their own rosters, and all 34 are compared in the tables below (Guadalupe's mail lists are scanned images, so only its in-person rosters are compared). The other 16 (Nueces, Lubbock, Hays, Ellis, Johnson, Kaufman, Comal, Midland, Parker, Ector, Taylor, Rockwall, Liberty, Orange, Coryell, Walker) link to the SOS portal;
- the SOS portal's per-county early voting CSVs for October 14-26, for all 50 counties, all days re-downloaded on the evening of October 27, since the state revises them;
- the SOS turnout tables for October 19-26.

**Method.** All files were read with Election Explorer's roster loader, which normalizes each county's layout to VUID, precinct, name, voting method and date. The loader now reads Henderson's new layout, Tom Green's untitled mail list and Guadalupe's in-person rosters, which earlier reviews read from the PDFs' text or compared separately. For every county file, the number of VUIDs loaded matches an independent count of the 10-digit identifiers in the raw file exactly for 358 of the 361 files. Of the other three, two differ by one identifier each; in Guadalupe's October 26 roster the loader finds 18 VUIDs the independent count misses (the PDF prints them run together with the ZIP code) and keeps four rows without their VUID (lines whose long address or line break pushes the VUID out of its column). Angelina's lists were taken from its cumulative October 25 file and its October 26 file. The lists were then compared by VUID:

- *early in-person, by day*: the county's in-person voters for each of October 19-26 vs. the SOS's IN-PERSON voters for that date. For counties whose lists are cumulative and undated (Tarrant, Hidalgo, Denton, El Paso, Potter), each day was recovered from consecutive daily snapshots (a day's voters = that day's snapshot minus the previous day's). A day counts toward the totals only when both sources have it and the state's file holds at least 90% of the county's count;
- *mail*: the county's latest mail list (or all daily files) vs. the SOS's MAIL-IN voters for October 14-26.

"County-only" and "state-only" voters were checked against the other 49 counties' files. Name and precinct comparisons ignore formatting (case, spacing, leading zeros, "Precinct"/"P"/"S" prefixes, and a two-digit precinct split such as "418603" for precinct 4186). Voter names and VUIDs are deliberately not reproduced in this report; the companion supplement lists the VUIDs and file locations of the anomalies for county and state staff.

## 3. The Secretary of State portal

**Where:** "Election Information & Turnout Data", `https://goelect.txelections.civixapps.com/ivis-evr-ui/evr`. Pick an election and an early voting date, then *Submit*: a table of registered voters, in-person and mail turnout per county, with a **CSV Download** per county under *Voter Details Report*.

**How the data is served.** The page fetches JSON whose `upload` field is the file in base64:

| Data | Request |
|---|---|
| Elections, early voting dates and county ids | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFile?type=EVR_ELECTION` |
| Turnout table for one date | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFile?type=EVR_EARLYVOTING&electionId=53815&electionDate=10/26/2026` |
| One county's voter CSV for one date | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFileByFormat?type=EVR_EARLYVOTING&electionId=53815&countyId=<id>&county=<NAME>&electionDate=<MM/DD/YYYY>&format=CSV` |

County ids follow alphabetical order, except that the portal sorts McLennan after McCulloch (id 161). An empty response means the county reported nothing for that day.

**File contents.** `VOTER_NAME` ("LAST, FIRST MIDDLE"), `ID_VOTER` (VUID), `VOTING_METHOD` (`IN-PERSON` / `MAIL-IN`), `PRECINCT`. There is **no address and no date column**. A county CSV holds only the voters credited on that date.

**Timeliness and revisions.** A day's files fill in over the following day or two. By the evening of October 27, all 50 counties had an in-person file for each of October 19-25, none of them partial against the county's own count, and 49 had one for October 26 (none for Webb); Harris's October 26 file holds 42,430 of the county's 79,474 voters. Re-downloading every day for every county on October 27 changed 141 files for October 14-22 besides the recent days. Compared with the files used in the October 26 review, 184 county-day counts for October 14-25 differ: 133 mail counts (net +1,094 voters, 65 of them downward) and 51 in-person counts (net +30, 25 downward). The state re-dates mail ballots as well as adding them: Brazoria's October 20 mail rose from 402 to 1,190; Kaufman's October 20 fell from 410 to 68, its October 21 from 178 to 62 and its October 22 from 56 to 6, while its October 15-18 files appeared for the first time (484 voters); Randall's October 22 rose from 570 to 711, Williamson's from 708 to 835; Comal's October 15 fell from 202 to 119. The October 26 files themselves grew between the afternoon and evening downloads of October 27: Collin from 15,259 to 30,506 records, Bexar from 40,435 to 44,969 and Dallas from 44,213 to 45,150.

**Mail dates.** The date of an SOS mail file is not the day the county received the ballot, and the state moves ballots from one day's file to another. In Travis, the state's October 16 mail voters are almost all ballots the county lists as received October 13, and its October 19 mail voters mostly the county's October 14 ballots. Mail comparisons in this review are therefore made over all days together.

**Turnout table vs. CSVs.** The statewide table now shows 784,344 in-person voters for October 19, 745,191 for October 20, 682,359 for October 21, 633,601 for October 22, 698,852 for October 23, 517,982 for October 24, 268,205 for October 25 and 539,751 for October 26. It agrees with the state's CSVs for every county and day except Travis (October 23: table 50,268, CSV 40,484), Ellis (October 23: 8,108 vs. 6,326), Hays (October 22: 10,862 vs. 8,792), Coryell (October 20: 1,867 vs. 1,442) and Henderson (October 25: 513 vs. 441).

**Name formatting.** The SOS appends name suffixes to the middle name without a space ("RAYJR"), which causes apparent name mismatches against county files.

## 6. Anomalies between county and state data

### 6.1 State county files include voters of other counties

Across October 14-26, 1,778 voters (1,753 in-person, 25 mail) in the SOS county files appear on a different county's own roster, with the same first name and that county's precinct. The largest numbers are in the SOS files for Travis (191), Dallas (167), Denton (163), Bexar (157), Collin (99), Hays (84), Johnson (75), Tarrant (67), Harris (66) and Brazos (64). No voter appears in two SOS county files, so these are records assigned to the wrong county's file, not duplicates. Anyone using SOS county files alone will slightly overcount each county.

### 6.2 State files identify voters Tarrant County redacts

Tarrant replaces the name, VUID and address of protected voters with "--Redacted--", keeping only precinct and date: 233 records in its current files (186 mail, 47 early in-person). No other county redacts identities; Harris hides the address (only) of 2,743 named voters.

- **Mail:** the SOS Tarrant mail files list 148 mail voters who are not on Tarrant's mail roster (after excluding one voter of another county). For 129 of them there is a redacted Tarrant mail record with the same precinct and the same date.
- **In person:** the SOS Tarrant files list 79 in-person voters (besides 66 of other counties) who are not on Tarrant's roster on any day; 46 of them match a redacted Tarrant in-person record by precinct.
- **Fewer redacted records each day:** Tarrant's cumulative in-person list held 104 redacted records on October 20, 99 on October 21, 79 on October 22, 69 on October 23, 62 on October 24, 56 on October 25, 55 on October 26 and 47 on October 27; its cumulative mail list held 215 in the October 23 afternoon file, 202 in the evening file, 192 on October 25 and 186 on October 27. A cumulative list should not shrink.

With eight days of data, the match remains close for both voting methods. This continues to indicate that the state publishes the name, VUID and precinct of voters whose identities Tarrant withholds. It cannot be proven record by record, because the redacted rows carry no identifier. Neither source exposes an address.

### 6.3 Reporting gaps in the state data

- **In-person:** every county has a state file for every day of October 19-25, all complete against the counties' own lists. For October 26 Webb has no state file and Harris's is partial (42,430 of 79,474). Two earlier days remain partial: Gregg's October 19 (2 of 3,677) and Hunt's October 20 (1,724 of 2,044).
- **Mail:** Brazos (9 of 1,622) and Ector (2) report almost none to the state.
- **Turnout table vs. CSV:** Travis, Ellis, Hays, Coryell and Henderson (§3).

### 6.4 Mail ballots in state data but not in the county's mail list

- **Dallas:** 1,172 state mail voters (besides one voter of another county) are not on Dallas's October 26 mail list; 927 of them are dated October 26 by the state, while the county's list holds 119 ballots received that day.
- **Collin:** 800 (710 dated October 26 and 71 October 25 by the state) are not on its list "through October 26", whose latest ballots are dated October 25.
- **Other lists not yet updated:** Victoria 62 (41 dated October 26, 14 October 24; its mail ballots stop at October 23), Tom Green 45 (35 dated October 26; its list's latest ballots are dated October 24), Wichita 153 (129 dated October 26) and Denton 114 (98 dated October 26) include ballots dated after the county's latest list.

## 8. Recommendations

1. **Download daily** through Election Day: Tarrant, Hidalgo, Denton, El Paso and Potter (no voting date per voter), Victoria, Tom Green, Jefferson and McLennan (cumulative files replaced), and Collin, Williamson, Cameron, Dallas, Fort Bend and Harris (latest file only). Save same-named files with the download time.
2. **Prefer county files** for counts and voter lists; use the SOS portal for the 16 counties that post nothing else and as a cross-check. Re-download every SOS day before each comparison, since the state fills in, revises and re-dates them, and do not read SOS mail dates as received dates or the turnout table as a substitute for the county CSVs.
3. **When using SOS county files,** expect voters of other counties in each file (§6.1) and check them against neighboring counties before treating them as anomalies.
4. **Refer to the counties:**
   - the nine voters recorded in two counties (§6.8);
   - the 18 voters with both a mail ballot and an in-person vote, first the three who voted in person on October 26;
   - the shared VUIDs (§6.7), first those on Bell's roster;
   - Williamson's mail precincts since October 22, and Bowie's October 20, 21 and 23 precincts;
   - Angelina's repeated check-ins in the files posted October 27;
   - Hunt's mail ballots in its in-person lists;
   - Bexar's mail ballots received twice;
   - the precinct differences in Harris, Tarrant, Galveston, El Paso and Bexar;
   - the mail lists that trail the state's (Dallas, Collin, Victoria, Tom Green).
5. **Refer to Tarrant County and the Secretary of State:**
   - the publication by the SOS of voters Tarrant redacts, and the shrinking counts of redacted records (§6.2);
   - the SOS files' inclusion of other counties' voters (§6.1), including the votes filed under a second county in §6.8;
   - the re-dating of mail ballots between daily files (§3);
   - the turnout table's Travis, Ellis, Hays, Coryell and Henderson figures (§3).
6. **Repeat this comparison** after early voting closes and after the canvass.

## Appendix A. Files

Downloaded files are saved per county:

- county files are saved as `G26_<county file name>`; files a county posts under the same name every day get the download date (`_2026-10-27`), or the download date and time (`_2026-10-27-14-43-12`) for cumulative lists that carry no voting dates (Hidalgo, Potter);
- SOS files are saved as `G26_<COUNTY>.2026 NOVEMBER GENERAL ELECTION.EarlyVoting.<MM_DD_YYYY>.csv`; when the state revises a day, the earlier copy is kept.

The files load in Election Explorer (File → Load Voter Roster); the comparisons in this report were produced from the loader's normalized output. The companion supplement (`Texas_Early_Voting_Roster_Review_2026-10-27_Supplement`) lists each anomalous record's VUID and its location in the county's and the state's files.

## Appendix B. Caveats

- In-person comparisons cover October 19-26; where the state's file for a day is partial or missing, that day is shown but left out of the totals.
- County files were downloaded October 20-27 at different times; several counties post same-day lists that are later replaced, and counties keep revising earlier days. The state files were re-downloaded about eight hours after the county files on October 27.
- Day-level counts for cumulative, undated county lists were recovered from consecutive snapshots and attribute voters added late to the day they first appear (Tarrant, §5.1).
- A match between two records means the same VUID; name and precinct differences were checked separately. A match by name, precinct or date without a common VUID (§6.2, §6.11) is an inference, not a confirmed identity.
- This review describes data as published. It does not establish that any voter voted improperly; the records flagged in §6.5-§6.10 have ordinary clerical explanations as well as serious ones, and only the counties can resolve them.
