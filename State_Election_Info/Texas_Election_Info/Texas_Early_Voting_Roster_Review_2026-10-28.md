# Texas Early Voting Rosters: County and State Data Review

**Election:** November 3, 2026 General Election\
**Data through:** Tuesday, October 27, 2026 (ninth day of in-person early voting)\
**Prepared:** October 28, 2026 (updates the reviews of October 20-27; county files as posted by 1:44 PM October 28, including October 27 lists that counties posted the evening of October 27, with Wichita's latest files downloaded by hand that morning; state files as posted by 8:58 PM October 28)\
**Scope:** the 50 most populous Texas counties and the Texas Secretary of State (SOS) roster portal

## 1. Summary

Texas Election Code §87.121 requires each county to post its early voting rosters (in-person and by mail) daily and to submit them for statewide elections to the Secretary of State, which republishes them. This update compares the county and state lists voter by voter (by Voter Unique Identifier, VUID) for the first nine days of in-person voting, October 19-27, in the 50 most populous counties.

Key findings:

1. **The two sources agree closely, but not completely, in every county.** Over every day for which both are complete, the county's and the state's lists of early in-person voters agree on 99.0% or more of voters in 32 of the 33 counties with dated county rosters, and on 98.7% in Guadalupe. In none of them do the two lists match exactly: every county has at least one voter on one list and not the other. (In this review a 100% figure is shown only when no voter is on one list and not the other.) Nine voters are an in-person voter in one source and a mail voter in the other (Hunt 5, and one each in Collin, Hidalgo, Cameron and Tom Green).
2. **October 27 is in for every county, and only Harris's file is partial.** All 50 counties have a state in-person file for each of October 19-27. Every file for October 19-26 is complete against the counties' own lists, now that the state has filled in Harris's October 26 (79,420 of the county's 79,474) and posted Webb's (3,446). For October 27, Harris's file holds 58,858 of the county's 75,248 voters; every other county's holds at least 90% of the county's count.
3. **The state keeps revising earlier days.** Since the evening of October 27, 192 county-day counts for October 14-26 changed in the state's files. 130 are mail counts (net -343 voters, 78 of them downward). The 62 in-person counts are net +40,690, almost all of it Harris's October 26 file filled in (+36,990) and Webb's posted (3,446); the other 60 net +254. The state moves mail ballots between days: Brazoria's October 20 mail rose from 1,190 to 1,651 while its October 19 fell from 249 to 5; Bastrop's October 20 rose from 389 to 742 while its October 19, 23 and 26 fell to 1 or 2; Williamson's October 23 fell from 504 to 125. The state also filled in its October 27 files during the day: Collin's grew from 5,332 to 28,304 records between the afternoon and evening downloads.
4. **Ten voters are recorded as voting in two counties.** New since the last review: one voter on Collin's in-person roster for October 23 and on Travis's for October 27 (same name). The state lists that voter only in its Travis October 23 file, with the Collin precinct. The other nine are unchanged (§6.8).
5. **Five more voters have both a mail ballot and an in-person vote, all voting in person on October 27:** three in Dallas, one in El Paso and one in Grayson, each with a mail ballot the county lists as received (October 5-24). Twenty-two voters are now listed both ways in the same county. The Denton case of the last review no longer appears: Denton's October 27 mail roster no longer lists that voter's mail ballot (§6.9).
6. **The turnout table still overstates Travis, Ellis, Hays, Coryell and Henderson.** For October 23 the portal's table shows 50,268 Travis in-person voters, against 40,484 in the state's own Travis file and 40,453 in Travis County's roster.
7. **Dallas's and Collin's mail lists have caught up.** Dallas's October 27 mail list now lacks 180 of the state's Dallas mail voters (1,172 a day earlier), and Collin's 291 (800). Newer lists that trail the state's: El Paso 276 (261 dated October 27 by the state; El Paso's latest list runs through October 26), Wichita 171 (115 dated October 27), Cameron 115 and Galveston 103 (91 each dated October 27).
8. **Two counties posted October 27 rosters that could not be read in full.** Guadalupe's October 27 in-person roster, posted October 28, is a new layout (a check-in report printed to PDF) that Election Explorer's loader does not yet read, so Guadalupe is compared for October 19-26. On one page of Gregg's October 27 list the rows are printed in pieces; the loader misses two voters there and pairs the page's names with the wrong VUIDs, so the name comparisons on that page (86 records) are left out.
9. **Bexar's October 27 check-in list is named for 2028** ("10272028"). It is read as October 27, 2026, and agrees with the state's file on 41,841 county and 41,940 state voters.
10. **Williamson's mail precincts are still wrong.** Its October 27 file gives a precinct different from the state's for 4,685 mail voters (and one in-person voter).
11. **Bowie's October 20, 21 and 23 reports disagree with the state** on the precinct of 4,718 voters; its other reports match.
12. **The state's county files contain voters of other counties.** Across October 14-27, 1,988 voters (1,962 in-person, 26 mail) appear in an SOS county file while being on a different county's own roster, with that county's precinct.
13. **The state's files continue to name voters that Tarrant County redacts.** Tarrant's cumulative in-person list, which held fewer redacted records each day from 104 on October 20 to 47 in the afternoon file of October 27, held 53 in that evening's file.
14. **Records that warrant review by the counties** (none is proof of wrongdoing; most have clerical explanations):
    - 22 voters listed both as a returned mail ballot and as an early in-person voter in the same county (Dallas 8, El Paso 7, Grayson 2, Tarrant 2, Bastrop, Jefferson and Travis 1 each);
    - 45 VUIDs that name entirely different people in two records, 10 of them on Bell's roster;
    - 165 same-day duplicate check-ins in 11 counties (132 of them in Angelina), and 27 mail ballots listed as received on two days (11 in Bexar);
    - precinct differences besides Williamson and Bowie: Harris 1,606, Tarrant 211, Galveston 165, El Paso 130, Bexar 69, Smith 69.

## 2. Scope and method

**Counties (by 2025 Census population):**

- Ranks 1-20: Harris, Dallas, Tarrant, Bexar, Travis, Collin, Denton, Fort Bend, Hidalgo, El Paso, Montgomery, Williamson, Cameron, Brazoria, Bell, Galveston, Nueces, Lubbock, Hays, Webb.
- Ranks 21-50: McLennan, Jefferson, Smith, Brazos, Ellis, Johnson, Kaufman, Comal, Guadalupe, Midland, Parker, Ector, Grayson, Randall, Taylor, Rockwall, Wichita, Gregg, Hunt, Liberty, Tom Green, Bastrop, Potter, Bowie, Victoria, Henderson, Angelina, Orange, Coryell, Walker.

**Sources:**

- each county's own roster files, downloaded daily October 20-28. 34 counties post their own rosters, and all 34 are compared in the tables below (Guadalupe's mail lists are scanned images, so only its in-person rosters are compared). The other 16 (Nueces, Lubbock, Hays, Ellis, Johnson, Kaufman, Comal, Midland, Parker, Ector, Taylor, Rockwall, Liberty, Orange, Coryell, Walker) link to the SOS portal;
- the SOS portal's per-county early voting CSVs for October 14-27, for all 50 counties, all days re-downloaded on the evening of October 28, since the state revises them (a second complete pass found no further change);
- the SOS turnout tables for October 19-27.

**Method.** All files were read with Election Explorer's roster loader, which normalizes each county's layout to VUID, precinct, name, voting method and date. For every county file, the number of VUIDs loaded matches an independent count of the 10-digit identifiers in the raw file exactly for 382 of the 387 files read. Of the other five: Dallas's and El Paso's October 27 files each hold one 10-digit number in an address, not a voter; one Travis mail workbook differs by one identifier, as in earlier reviews; in Guadalupe's October 26 roster the loader finds 18 VUIDs the independent count misses (the PDF prints them run together with the ZIP code) and keeps four rows without their VUID (a long address pushes it out of its column); and in Gregg's October 27 list the loader misses two voters on one page, where the PDF prints rows in pieces (§1, item 8). Guadalupe's October 27 roster, in a new layout, could not be read. Three corrections were made before comparing: Bexar's October 27 list, named "10272028", is dated October 27, 2026; Tom Green's roster posted October 28 (named "102626") holds 373 voters of October 27, part of that day, which are left out; and 86 name-comparison records from the misread Gregg page are dropped. Angelina's lists were taken from its cumulative October 25 file and its October 26 file. The lists were then compared by VUID:

- *early in-person, by day*: the county's in-person voters for each of October 19-27 vs. the SOS's IN-PERSON voters for that date. For counties whose lists are cumulative and undated (Tarrant, Hidalgo, Denton, El Paso, Potter), each day was recovered from consecutive daily snapshots (a day's voters = that day's snapshot minus the previous day's). A day counts toward the totals only when both sources have it and the state's file holds at least 90% of the county's count;
- *mail*: the county's latest mail list (or all daily files) vs. the SOS's MAIL-IN voters for October 14-27.

"County-only" and "state-only" voters were checked against the other 49 counties' files. Name and precinct comparisons ignore formatting (case, spacing, leading zeros, "Precinct"/"P"/"S" prefixes, and a two-digit precinct split such as "418603" for precinct 4186). Voter names and VUIDs are deliberately not reproduced in this report; the companion supplement lists the VUIDs and file locations of the anomalies for county and state staff.

## 3. The Secretary of State portal

**Where:** "Election Information & Turnout Data", `https://goelect.txelections.civixapps.com/ivis-evr-ui/evr`. Pick an election and an early voting date, then *Submit*: a table of registered voters, in-person and mail turnout per county, with a **CSV Download** per county under *Voter Details Report*.

**How the data is served.** The page fetches JSON whose `upload` field is the file in base64:

| Data | Request |
|---|---|
| Elections, early voting dates and county ids | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFile?type=EVR_ELECTION` |
| Turnout table for one date | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFile?type=EVR_EARLYVOTING&electionId=53815&electionDate=10/27/2026` |
| One county's voter CSV for one date | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFileByFormat?type=EVR_EARLYVOTING&electionId=53815&countyId=<id>&county=<NAME>&electionDate=<MM/DD/YYYY>&format=CSV` |

County ids follow alphabetical order, except that the portal sorts McLennan after McCulloch (id 161). An empty response means the county reported nothing for that day.

**File contents.** `VOTER_NAME` ("LAST, FIRST MIDDLE"), `ID_VOTER` (VUID), `VOTING_METHOD` (`IN-PERSON` / `MAIL-IN`), `PRECINCT`. There is **no address and no date column**. A county CSV holds only the voters credited on that date.

**Timeliness and revisions.** A day's files fill in over the following day or two. By the evening of October 28, all 50 counties had an in-person file for each of October 19-27; none of the files for October 19-26 is partial against the county's own count, and for October 27 only Harris's is (58,858 of 75,248). Nueces's October 27 file holds 1,889 voters, against 6,151 for October 26; Nueces posts no roster of its own to compare. Re-downloading every day for every county on the evening of October 28 changed 249 files for October 14-27 from the afternoon download. Compared with the files used in the October 27 review, 192 county-day counts for October 14-26 differ: 130 mail counts (net -343 voters, 78 of them downward) and 62 in-person counts (net +40,690, of which Harris's October 26 file +36,990 and Webb's newly posted October 26 file 3,446; 7 downward). The state re-dates mail ballots as well as adding them: Brazoria's October 20 mail rose from 1,190 to 1,651 and its October 26 from 184 to 382, while its October 19 fell from 249 to 5 and its October 15 from 59 to 3; Bastrop's October 20 rose from 389 to 742, while its October 19, 23 and 26 fell from 55, 74 and 77 to 1 or 2; Lubbock's October 20 rose from 57 to 354; Williamson's October 23 fell from 504 to 125; Jefferson's October 26 fell from 215 to 47; Comal's October 15 fell from 119 to 8. The October 27 files themselves grew between the afternoon and evening downloads of October 28: Collin from 5,332 to 28,304 records, Comal from 6,086 to 6,373 and Cameron from 5,552 to 5,635.

**Mail dates.** The date of an SOS mail file is not the day the county received the ballot, and the state moves ballots from one day's file to another. In Travis, the state's October 16 mail voters are almost all ballots the county lists as received October 13, and its October 19 mail voters mostly the county's October 14 ballots. Mail comparisons in this review are therefore made over all days together.

**Turnout table vs. CSVs.** The statewide table now shows 784,391 in-person voters for October 19, 745,469 for October 20, 683,419 for October 21, 633,676 for October 22, 698,904 for October 23, 518,051 for October 24, 268,407 for October 25, 582,983 for October 26 and 531,399 for October 27. It agrees with the state's CSVs for every county and day except Travis (October 23: table 50,268, CSV 40,484), Ellis (October 23: 8,108 vs. 6,326), Hays (October 22: 10,861 vs. 8,791), Coryell (October 20: 1,867 vs. 1,442) and Henderson (October 25: 513 vs. 441).

**Name formatting.** The SOS appends name suffixes to the middle name without a space ("RAYJR"), which causes apparent name mismatches against county files.

## 6. Anomalies between county and state data

### 6.1 State county files include voters of other counties

Across October 14-27, 1,988 voters (1,962 in-person, 26 mail) in the SOS county files appear on a different county's own roster, with the same first name and that county's precinct. The largest numbers are in the SOS files for Travis (207), Dallas (190), Denton (179), Bexar (166), Collin (110), Hays (88), Johnson (85), Tarrant (80), Harris (77) and Parker (73). No voter appears in two SOS county files, so these are records assigned to the wrong county's file, not duplicates. Anyone using SOS county files alone will slightly overcount each county.

### 6.2 State files identify voters Tarrant County redacts

Tarrant replaces the name, VUID and address of protected voters with "--Redacted--", keeping only precinct and date: 235 records in its current files (182 mail, 53 early in-person). No other county redacts identities; Harris hides the address (only) of 2,927 named voters.

- **Mail:** the SOS Tarrant mail files list 148 mail voters who are not on Tarrant's mail roster (after excluding one voter of another county). For 128 of them there is a redacted Tarrant mail record with the same precinct and the same date.
- **In person:** the SOS Tarrant files list 89 in-person voters (besides 79 of other counties) who are not on Tarrant's roster on any day; 52 of them match a redacted Tarrant in-person record by precinct.
- **Redacted counts:** Tarrant's cumulative in-person list held 104 redacted records on October 20, falling each day to 47 in the afternoon file of October 27, then 53 in the evening file of October 27 (the list through October 27); its cumulative mail list held 215 in the October 23 afternoon file, 202 in the evening file, 192 on October 25, 186 on October 27 and 182 in the October 27 evening file. A cumulative list should not shrink.

With nine days of data, the match remains close for both voting methods. This continues to indicate that the state publishes the name, VUID and precinct of voters whose identities Tarrant withholds. It cannot be proven record by record, because the redacted rows carry no identifier. Neither source exposes an address.

### 6.3 Reporting gaps in the state data

- **In-person:** every county has a state file for every day of October 19-27; all files for October 19-26 are complete against the counties' own lists, and for October 27 Harris's is partial (58,858 of 75,248). Two earlier days remain partial: Gregg's October 19 (2 of 3,677) and Hunt's October 20 (1,724 of 2,044). Nueces's October 25 and 27 files are small (534 and 1,889 voters).
- **Mail:** Brazos (9 of 1,696) and Ector (2) report almost none to the state.
- **Turnout table vs. CSV:** Travis, Ellis, Hays, Coryell and Henderson (§3).

## 8. Recommendations

1. **Download daily** through Election Day: Tarrant, Hidalgo, Denton, El Paso and Potter (no voting date per voter), Victoria, Tom Green, Jefferson and McLennan (cumulative files replaced), and Collin, Williamson, Cameron, Dallas, Fort Bend and Harris (latest file only). 2. **Prefer county files** for counts and voter lists; use the SOS portal for the 16 counties that post nothing else and as a cross-check. Re-download every SOS day before each comparison, since the state fills in, revises and re-dates them, and do not read SOS mail dates as received dates or the turnout table as a substitute for the county CSVs.
3. **When using SOS county files,** expect voters of other counties in each file (§6.1) and check them against neighboring counties before treating them as anomalies.
4. **Refer to the counties:**
   - the ten voters recorded in two counties (§6.8), first the Collin/Travis voter of October 23 and 27;
   - the 22 voters with both a mail ballot and an in-person vote, first the five who voted in person on October 27;
   - the shared VUIDs (§6.7), first those on Bell's roster;
   - Williamson's mail precincts since October 22, and Bowie's October 20, 21 and 23 precincts;
   - Angelina's repeated check-ins in the files posted October 27;
   - Hunt's mail ballots in its in-person lists;
   - Bexar's mail ballots received twice;
   - the precinct differences in Harris, Tarrant, Galveston, El Paso, Bexar and Smith;
   - the mail lists that trail the state's (El Paso, Wichita, Cameron, Galveston, Gregg, Victoria).
5. **Refer to Tarrant County and the Secretary of State:**
   - the publication by the SOS of voters Tarrant redacts, and the changing counts of redacted records (§6.2);
   - the SOS files' inclusion of other counties' voters (§6.1), including the votes filed under a second county in §6.8;
   - the re-dating of mail ballots between daily files (§3);
   - the turnout table's Travis, Ellis, Hays, Coryell and Henderson figures (§3).
6. **Repeat this comparison** after early voting closes and after the canvass.

## Appendix A. Files

Downloaded files are saved per county:

- county files are saved as `G26_<county file name>`; files a county posts under the same name every day get the download date (`_2026-10-28`), or the download date and time (`_2026-10-28-13-10-07`) for cumulative lists that carry no voting dates (Hidalgo, Potter);
- SOS files are saved as `G26_<COUNTY>.2026 NOVEMBER GENERAL ELECTION.EarlyVoting.<MM_DD_YYYY>.csv`; when the state revises a day, the earlier copy is kept.

The files load in Election Explorer (File → Load Voter Roster); the comparisons in this report were produced from the loader's normalized output. The companion supplement (`Texas_Early_Voting_Roster_Review_2026-10-28_Supplement`) lists each anomalous record's VUID and its location in the county's and the state's files.

## Appendix B. Caveats

- In-person comparisons cover October 19-27; where the state's file for a day is partial or missing, that day is shown but left out of the totals.
- County files were downloaded October 20-28 at different times; several counties post same-day lists that are later replaced, and counties keep revising earlier days. The state files were re-downloaded about seven hours after the county files on October 28.
- Day-level counts for cumulative, undated county lists were recovered from consecutive snapshots and attribute voters added late to the day they first appear (Tarrant, §5.1).
- A match between two records means the same VUID; name and precinct differences were checked separately. A match by name, precinct or date without a common VUID (§6.2, §6.11) is an inference, not a confirmed identity.
- This review describes data as published. It does not establish that any voter voted improperly; the records flagged in §6.5-§6.10 have ordinary clerical explanations as well as serious ones, and only the counties can resolve them.
