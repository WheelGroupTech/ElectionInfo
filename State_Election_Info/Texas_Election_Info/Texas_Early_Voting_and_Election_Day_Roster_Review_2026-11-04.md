# Texas Early Voting and Election Day Rosters: County and State Data Review

**Election:** November 3, 2026 General Election\
**Data:** early voting October 19-30, mail ballots through Election Day, and Election Day, November 3, 2026\
**Prepared:** November 4, 2026 (updates the reviews of October 20 - November 2; county files as posted by 3:30 PM November 4, with Travis's ZIP and Wichita's Election Day check-ins downloaded by hand that afternoon; state files as posted on the afternoon of November 4)\
**Scope:** the 50 most populous Texas counties and the Texas Secretary of State (SOS) roster portal

## 1. Summary

Texas Election Code §87.121 requires each county to post its early voting rosters (in-person and by mail) and, after the election, its Election Day roster, and to submit them for statewide elections to the Secretary of State, which republishes them. This review compares the county and state lists voter by voter (by Voter Unique Identifier, VUID) for all twelve days of in-person early voting, October 19-30, for mail ballots through Election Day, and for Election Day, November 3, in the 50 most populous counties. It was prepared the day after the election: several counties had not yet posted their Election Day rosters, and most of the largest had not yet sent them to the state.

Key findings:

1. **Nineteen of the 34 counties that post their own rosters had posted an Election Day roster by the afternoon of November 4,** listing 1,165,836 Election Day voters: Harris 306,131, Dallas 159,591, Bexar 139,088, Tarrant 137,270, Travis 101,814, Collin 76,634, Williamson 46,313, Hidalgo 38,642, and Brazoria, Galveston, Smith, Brazos, Jefferson, Webb, Grayson, Bastrop, Wichita, Gregg and Potter (§5.4). Fifteen had not: Denton, Fort Bend, El Paso, Montgomery, Cameron, Bell, McLennan, Hunt, Tom Green, Bowie, Victoria, Henderson, Angelina, Randall and Guadalupe. The "Election Day Cumulative Roster" on Victoria's roster page is from the May 26, 2026 runoff.
2. **The state's Election Day files are far from complete.** The state's Election Day turnout table shows 651,633 Election Day voters statewide. Harris's state file holds 38 voters, Bexar's 21 and Dallas's 95, against 306,131, 139,088 and 159,591 on the counties' rosters; Tarrant's holds 13% of the county's count, Travis's 45%, Collin's 68% and Williamson's 88%; Nueces's and Bastrop's state files are empty. Where the state's file is at least 90% complete (Hidalgo, Brazoria, Galveston, Smith, Brazos, Jefferson, Webb, Gregg, Potter), the county and state lists agree on 99.2% to 99.96% of Election Day voters.
3. **Seventeen voters are listed as voting on Election Day and earlier in the same county's own files:** 7 also voted early in person (Bexar 3, Grayson 3, Galveston 1; the state lists the early vote for all seven), and 10 also have a returned mail ballot (Dallas 5, Webb 2, and one each in Tarrant, Travis and Brazos) (§6.12). Nine more Election Day voters are listed by the state as early voters in the same county, although the county's own early lists do not have them (Harris 4, and one each in Bexar, Collin, Williamson, Galveston and Smith) (§5.4).
4. **Thirty-one voters are now recorded as voting in two counties, eleven of them new and all involving Election Day.** Seven voted early in one county and on Election Day in another (Denton then Smith, Travis then Bexar, Bexar then Dallas, El Paso then Galveston, Harris then Travis, Hidalgo then Travis, and Tarrant then Bexar), three voted on Election Day in two counties (Bexar and Harris, Dallas and Travis, Collin and Dallas), and one cast an Election Day provisional ballot in Collin and voted on Election Day in Dallas. Ten of the eleven have the same first and last name in both counties; the Tarrant/Bexar voter has the same first name and a different last name (§6.8).
5. **The state keeps revising the early voting files, and now lists early voters the counties do not.** Since the November 2 review, 395 county-day counts for October 14-30 changed: 271 mail counts (net +3,641, 154 of them downward) and 124 in-person counts (net +1,623, 45 downward). Hays's mail ballots were re-dated in bulk (October 21 from 53 to 1,252, October 28 from 94 to 753); Harris's October 30 mail file, empty until now, holds 1,146. The state's files now list early in-person voters that appear on none of the county's lists: Bexar 1,843, Collin 1,073 (and 682 more that Collin lists only as provisional voters), Travis 513, Fort Bend 455 and Victoria 90, most of them dated October 27-30.
6. **Early in-person voting: the two sources still agree closely, but not completely.** Over every day for which both are complete, the county's and the state's lists of early in-person voters agree on 99.0% or more of voters in 30 of the 33 counties with dated county rosters, and on 98.9% in Fort Bend and Hunt and 98.6% in Guadalupe. Bexar's agreement fell from 99.7% to 99.5% with the state's revisions. The state's early voting turnout table now agrees with its CSVs for every county and day; the overstatements reported earlier (Travis, Ellis, Hays, Coryell, Henderson, Orange, Potter, Smith) are gone.
7. **Mail ballots through Election Day.** The state's Election Day files add mail ballots, and several county mail lists stop before Election Day. Travis's list lacks 579 state mail voters (350 of them in the state's Election Day file), Montgomery's 527 (355; its latest list runs through October 30), Harris's 373, Hunt's 344, Galveston's 318 (it has posted no mail list for October 27 or 29), Dallas's 277, Gregg's 199, Bexar's 194, Jefferson's 190 (184 in the state's Election Day file) and Wichita's 169.
8. **The state's files continue to name voters that Tarrant County redacts,** now including Election Day: Tarrant's Election Day roster has 76 redacted records. The state's partial Tarrant Election Day file also lists 816 voters who are on none of Tarrant's lists.
9. **Records that warrant review by the counties** (none is proof of wrongdoing; most have clerical explanations):
    - the 17 voters with an Election Day vote and an earlier vote in the same county, and the 31 voters on two counties' rosters, first the eleven new ones;
    - 43 voters listed both as a returned mail ballot and as an early in-person voter in the same county (Dallas 16, El Paso 8, Tarrant 5, Jefferson 4, Grayson 3, Tom Green 2, Travis 2, and one each in Bexar, Brazoria and Galveston);
    - 80 VUIDs that name entirely different people in two records, now including one VUID on two rows of Bexar's Election Day roster and one on Collin's and Dallas's Election Day rosters;
    - 228 same-day duplicate check-ins (166 in Angelina; 31 on Election Day rosters, 10 of them Bexar's and 7 Jefferson's), and 39 mail ballots listed as received on two days;
    - precinct differences: Williamson's mail voters (4,718), Bowie's October 20, 21 and 23 reports (4,717), Harris 3,509, Tarrant 316, El Paso 253, Galveston 206, Brazos 164.

## 2. Scope and method

**Counties (by 2025 Census population):**

- Ranks 1-20: Harris, Dallas, Tarrant, Bexar, Travis, Collin, Denton, Fort Bend, Hidalgo, El Paso, Montgomery, Williamson, Cameron, Brazoria, Bell, Galveston, Nueces, Lubbock, Hays, Webb.
- Ranks 21-50: McLennan, Jefferson, Smith, Brazos, Ellis, Johnson, Kaufman, Comal, Guadalupe, Midland, Parker, Ector, Grayson, Randall, Taylor, Rockwall, Wichita, Gregg, Hunt, Liberty, Tom Green, Bastrop, Potter, Bowie, Victoria, Henderson, Angelina, Orange, Coryell, Walker.

**Sources:**

- each county's own roster files, downloaded daily October 20 - November 4. 34 counties post their own rosters, and all 34 are compared in the tables below (Guadalupe's mail lists are scanned images, so only its in-person rosters are compared). The other 16 (Nueces, Lubbock, Hays, Ellis, Johnson, Kaufman, Comal, Midland, Parker, Ector, Taylor, Rockwall, Liberty, Orange, Coryell, Walker) link to the SOS portal;
- the SOS portal's per-county early voting CSVs for October 14-30, for all 50 counties, all days re-downloaded on the afternoon of November 4, since the state revises them (a second complete pass found no further change);
- the SOS portal's per-county Election Day rosters (ZIPs, downloaded November 4; 48 counties have one, Nueces's and Bastrop's are empty);
- the SOS early voting turnout tables for October 19-30 and the Election Day turnout table.

**Method.** All files were read with Election Explorer's roster loader, which normalizes each county's layout to VUID, precinct, name, voting method and date. For every county file, the number of VUIDs loaded matches an independent count of the 10-digit identifiers in the raw file exactly for 493 of the 501 files read, including every Election Day roster (Bexar's is a 58 MB PDF). Of the other eight, two are the corrections listed below; Dallas's in-person CSV, El Paso's October 30 file and Bastrop's Election Day CSV each hold one 10-digit number in an address, not a voter; one Travis mail workbook differs by one identifier, as in earlier reviews; and in Guadalupe's October 26 and 27 rosters VUIDs printed together with the ZIP code are counted differently. Collin posted a combined early voting and Election Day list ("voters-through-november-3-2026") whose voting method is in a "VotedPhase" column the loader does not yet read; Collin's separate early voting and Election Day lists were used instead. Webb's October 28 mail list gives no VUIDs; its voters were matched by name to the state's Webb mail voters, which assigned VUIDs to 46 of its 52 rows. Corrections made before comparing: mail ballots the county lists as received November 4, after Election Day, are left out (Bastrop 8, Randall 5). The lists were then compared by VUID:

- *early in-person, by day*: the county's in-person voters for each of October 19-30 vs. the SOS's IN-PERSON voters for that date. For counties whose lists are cumulative and undated (Tarrant, Hidalgo, Denton, El Paso, Potter), each day was recovered from consecutive daily snapshots (a day's voters = that day's snapshot minus the previous day's). A day counts toward the totals only when both sources have it and the state's file holds at least 90% of the county's count;
- *mail*: the county's latest mail list (or all daily files) vs. the SOS's MAIL-IN voters in its early voting files for October 14-30 and its Election Day file;
- *Election Day*: the county's Election Day roster vs. the IN-PERSON voters of the SOS Election Day file. Harris's, Tarrant's, Hidalgo's and Potter's Election Day rosters carry no date per voter; every voter on them is an Election Day voter. Collin's Election Day roster also lists Election Day provisional ballots, which are counted separately.

"County-only" and "state-only" voters were checked against the other 49 counties' files. Name and precinct comparisons ignore formatting (case, spacing, leading zeros, "Precinct"/"P"/"S" prefixes, and a two-digit precinct split such as "418603" for precinct 4186). Voter names and VUIDs are deliberately not reproduced in this report; the companion supplement lists the VUIDs and file locations of the anomalies for county and state staff.

## 3. The Secretary of State portal

**Where:** "Election Information & Turnout Data", `https://goelect.txelections.civixapps.com/ivis-evr-ui/evr`. Pick an election and a date (an early voting date or Election Day), then *Submit*: a table of registered voters, in-person and mail turnout per county, with a download per county under *Voter Details Report*.

**How the data is served.** The page fetches JSON whose `upload` field is the file in base64:

| Data | Request |
|---|---|
| Elections, early voting dates and county ids | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFile?type=EVR_ELECTION` |
| Turnout table for one early voting date | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFile?type=EVR_EARLYVOTING&electionId=53815&electionDate=10/30/2026` |
| Election Day turnout table | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFile?type=EVR_ELECTIONDAYTURNOUT&electionId=53815&electionDate=11/03/2026` |
| One county's voter CSV for one early voting date | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFileByFormat?type=EVR_EARLYVOTING&electionId=53815&countyId=<id>&county=<NAME>&electionDate=<MM/DD/YYYY>&format=CSV` |
| One county's Election Day roster | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFileByFormat?type=EVR_ELECTIONDAYTURNOUT&electionId=53815&countyId=<id>&county=<NAME>&electionDate=11/03/2026&format=ZIP` (CSV is not offered) |

County ids follow alphabetical order, except that the portal sorts McLennan after McCulloch (id 161). An empty response means the county reported nothing for that day.

**File contents.** `VOTER_NAME` ("LAST, FIRST MIDDLE"), `ID_VOTER` (VUID), `VOTING_METHOD` (`IN-PERSON` / `MAIL-IN`), `PRECINCT`. There is **no address and no date column**. An early voting CSV holds only the voters credited on that date. The Election Day ZIP holds two CSVs: `<id>_<COUNTY>_VOTER_INFO.csv` (the voters, including mail ballots credited on Election Day) and `<id>_<COUNTY>_POLLING_PLACE_INFO.csv` (voter counts per polling place).

**Election Day files.** On the afternoon of November 4 the state's Election Day turnout table showed 651,633 in-person Election Day voters statewide and its Election Day files held 489,671 in-person voters for the 50 counties. Of the largest counties, Harris's file held 38 voters, Bexar's 21, Dallas's 95, El Paso's 5, Fort Bend's 4 and Denton's 32; Nueces's and Bastrop's were empty. Counties that post no roster of their own had sent the state their Election Day voters in full or in part: Hays 21,379, Lubbock 20,145, Ellis 18,459, Parker 16,936, Rockwall 9,002, Orange 6,903, Liberty 6,089, Coryell 5,098, Walker 4,957 and Johnson 3,906, but Comal 7, Kaufman 5, Midland 2, Taylor 1, Ector 1 and Nueces none (§5.3).

**Timeliness and revisions of the early voting files.** By November 4 all 50 counties had a complete in-person file for each of October 19-30 (every county's October 30 file holds at least 98.6% of the county's count, except Henderson's, whose October 30 list also holds mail voters, §5.1). Re-downloading every day for every county on November 4 changed 405 files from the download of November 2. Compared with the files used in the November 2 review, 395 county-day counts for October 14-30 differ: 271 mail counts (net +3,641 voters, 154 of them downward) and 124 in-person counts (net +1,623, 45 downward). The largest in-person changes are Bexar's October 30 (+378), Hays's October 30 (+300) and Collin's October 30 (+216); Bexar's earlier days fell by 50-100 each (October 19 from 42,070 to 41,979). The state re-dates mail ballots as well as adding them: Hays's October 21 mail rose from 53 to 1,252, its October 28 from 94 to 753 and its October 14 from 559 to 1,133; Lubbock's October 27 rose from 77 to 528 while its October 19 fell from 159 to 24 and its October 21 from 148 to 19; Harris's October 30 mail file now holds 1,146 (none on November 2); Cameron's October 29 rose from 556 to 707 while its October 26 fell from 94 to 9.

**Early voters the counties do not list.** After these revisions the state's early voting files list in-person voters who are on none of the county's own lists, on any day and by any method: Bexar 1,843, Collin 1,073 (and 682 more whom Collin lists only as provisional voters), Travis 513, Fort Bend 455 (and 23 whom Fort Bend lists only as mail voters) and Victoria 90. Most are dated October 27-30 by the state (in Bexar, 470 on October 30). They are not voters of other counties.

**Mail dates.** The date of an SOS mail file is not the day the county received the ballot, and the state moves ballots from one day's file to another. Mail comparisons in this review are therefore made over all days together, including the mail ballots in the state's Election Day files.

**Turnout table vs. CSVs.** The statewide early voting table now shows 784,391 in-person voters for October 19, 743,682 for October 20, 684,257 for October 21, 630,948 for October 22, 690,956 for October 23, 518,894 for October 24, 268,355 for October 25, 584,002 for October 26, 550,795 for October 27, 549,738 for October 28, 590,799 for October 29 and 852,874 for October 30 (7,449,691 in all), and 260,372 mail voters. It now agrees with the state's CSVs for every county and day. The Election Day table's statewide totals (8,102,231 in-person voters for the election and 213,462 mail voters) do not match the early voting table's (7,449,691 early in-person voters plus 651,633 on Election Day is 8,101,324; 260,372 mail voters).

**Name formatting.** The SOS appends name suffixes to the middle name without a space ("RAYJR"), which causes apparent name mismatches against county files.

## 6. Anomalies between county and state data

### 6.1 State county files include voters of other counties

Across October 14 - November 3, 3,138 voters (2,872 early in-person, 226 on Election Day, 40 mail) in the SOS county files appear on a different county's own roster, with the same first name and that county's precinct. The largest numbers are in the SOS files for Travis (339), Dallas (290), Denton (260), Bexar (252), Harris (159), Collin (156), Johnson (139), Hays (138), Tarrant (117) and Brazos (114). Of the Harris, Dallas and Bexar state Election Day files, which hold only 38, 95 and 21 voters, 34, 24 and 13 are voters on another county's roster. No voter appears in two SOS county files, so these are records assigned to the wrong county's file, not duplicates. Anyone using SOS county files alone will slightly overcount each county.

### 6.2 State files identify voters Tarrant County redacts

Tarrant replaces the name, VUID and address of protected voters with "--Redacted--", keeping only precinct and date: 166 records in its current mail list, 50 in its early in-person list and 76 in its Election Day roster. No other county redacts identities; Harris hides the address (only) of named voters.

- **Mail:** the SOS Tarrant mail files list 136 mail voters who are not on Tarrant's mail roster (135 after excluding one voter of another county). For 102 of them there is a redacted Tarrant mail record with the same precinct and the same date.
- **Early in person:** the SOS Tarrant files list 97 in-person voters (besides 114 of other counties) who are not on Tarrant's roster on any day; 49 of them match a redacted Tarrant in-person record by precinct.
- **Election Day:** the state's Tarrant Election Day file, 13% complete, lists 816 voters who are on none of Tarrant's lists, more than ten times Tarrant's 76 redacted Election Day records.
- **Redacted counts:** Tarrant's cumulative in-person list held 104 redacted records on October 20, falling to 47 in the afternoon file of October 27, then 53 in the evening file of October 27, 51 on October 30 and 50 on October 31; its cumulative mail list held 215 in the October 23 afternoon file, falling to 168 on October 31 and 166 on November 4. A cumulative list should not shrink.

The match remains close for both early voting methods. This continues to indicate that the state publishes the name, VUID and precinct of voters whose identities Tarrant withholds. It cannot be proven record by record, because the redacted rows carry no identifier. Neither source exposes an address.

### 6.3 Reporting gaps in the state data

- **Election Day:** the state's Election Day files are partial or empty for most large counties (§3, §5.4): Harris, Dallas, Bexar, Denton, El Paso, Fort Bend, Cameron, Bell, McLennan, Grayson, Bastrop, Wichita, Victoria, Henderson, Angelina, Randall, Tom Green, Comal, Kaufman, Midland, Taylor, Ector and Nueces hold fewer than 100 Election Day voters each.
- **Early in-person:** every county has a complete state file for every day of October 19-30, but the state now lists early voters some counties do not (§3). Gregg's October 19 (3 of 3,677) and Hunt's October 20 (1,724 of 2,044) remain partial; Nueces's October 25 and 27 files are small (534 and 1,889 voters).
- **Mail:** Ector (2) reports almost none to the state; the state re-dated Hays's and Lubbock's mail ballots in bulk (§3).

### 6.5 Precinct differences

- **Williamson:** the file posted November 4, like every file since October 22, gives a precinct different from the state's for its mail voters (4,718); the October 21 file matched the state. Three early in-person rows and one Election Day row differ as well.
- **Bowie:** the October 20, 21 and 23 early vote reports give a precinct that differs from the state's for 1,566, 1,710 and 1,441 voters (4,717); the October 22 and 24-30 reports match.
- **Brazos:** 163 mail voters, 141 of them in its mail list of October 19-20, and one in-person voter.
- **Bexar:** 65 voters in the October 22 check-in report, and six in other reports.

### 6.6 Surnames

On matched voters, the surname differs between the county and the state in 186 Collin, 90 Tarrant, 89 Denton, 84 Bexar, 76 Williamson, 68 Galveston and 62 Brazoria records, and in 53 or fewer elsewhere (1,197 in all, including Election Day rosters). ## Appendix A. Files

Downloaded files are saved per county:

- county files are saved as `G26_<county file name>`; files a county posts under the same name every day get the download date (`_2026-11-04`), or the download date and time for cumulative lists that carry no voting dates (Hidalgo, Potter);
- SOS early voting files are saved as `G26_<COUNTY>.2026 NOVEMBER GENERAL ELECTION.EarlyVoting.<MM_DD_YYYY>.csv`, and SOS Election Day files as `G26_<COUNTY>.2026 NOVEMBER GENERAL ELECTION.ElectionDay.11_03_2026.zip`; when the state revises a day, the earlier copy is kept.

The files load in Election Explorer (File → Load Voter Roster); the comparisons in this report were produced from the loader's normalized output. The companion supplement (`Texas_Early_Voting_and_Election_Day_Roster_Review_2026-11-04_Supplement`) lists each anomalous record's VUID and its location in the county's and the state's files.

## Appendix B. Caveats

- In-person comparisons cover October 19-30 and November 3; where the state's file for a day is partial or missing, that day is shown but left out of the totals. Most state Election Day files were partial on November 4.
- County files were downloaded October 20 - November 4 at different times; several counties post same-day lists that are later replaced, and counties keep revising earlier days. The state files were re-downloaded on the afternoon of November 4.
- Day-level counts for cumulative, undated county lists were recovered from consecutive snapshots and attribute voters added late to the day they first appear (§5.1).
- A match between two records means the same VUID; name and precinct differences were checked separately. A match by name, precinct or date without a common VUID (§2, §6.2, §6.11) is an inference, not a confirmed identity.
- This review describes data as published. It does not establish that any voter voted improperly; the records flagged in §5.4 and §6.5-§6.12 have ordinary clerical explanations as well as serious ones, and only the counties can resolve them.
