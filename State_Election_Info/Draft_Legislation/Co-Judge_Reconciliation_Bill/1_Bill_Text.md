## A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
## AN ACT

relating to a bipartisan pre-canvass reconciliation of election records and a bounded,
non-destructive integrity examination of voting system equipment conducted under the custody of
the general custodian of election records; creating criminal offenses.

**BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:**

---

**SECTION 1. FINDINGS AND PURPOSE.** (a) The legislature finds that:

(1) public confidence in the outcome of an election depends on a transparent, verifiable
accounting of every ballot, every voter, and every piece of equipment and record used in the
election, and on the ability of each of the principal political parties to observe that
accounting;

(2) the law of this state already relies on party-balanced election bodies that resolve
disagreement without granting any member a veto, including:

> (A) the presiding judge and alternate presiding judge appointed from different political
> parties under Section 32.002, Election Code, with the presiding judge "in charge of and
> responsible for" the polling place under Section 32.071, Election Code, and the corresponding
> officers of a central counting station under Section 127.005, Election Code;
>
> (B) the early voting ballot board and signature verification committee under Chapter 87,
> Election Code, which fix a majority rule in advance, assign a presiding officer drawn from the
> party whose nominee for governor received the most votes in the county, apply a default
> outcome, and permit review in one direction only;
>
> (C) the hand-count procedures of Chapter 65, Election Code, under which disagreeing tally lists
> are resolved by recounting rather than by vote; and
>
> (D) the recount procedures of Chapter 213, Election Code, under which a recount committee chair
> decides counting questions with the authority of a presiding election judge and gives written
> reasons;

(3) federal law, including 52 U.S.C. Section 20701, requires officers of election to retain and
preserve election records, and guidance of the United States Department of Justice provides that
those officers must retain ultimate management authority over the retention and security of those
records;

(4) post-election reviews in which original ballots, records, or voting equipment have left the
custody of election officials have raised chain-of-custody, certification, security, and cost
concerns, and those concerns are avoided when every examination is conducted by or under the
authority of the official custodian on equipment the custodian controls;

(5) a reconciliation is most useful when it informs the canvass rather than reviewing a completed
one, and it must be completed within the period for the local canvass prescribed by
Section 67.003, Election Code, so that it does not delay the certification of results;

(6) the paper ballot is the record of the voter's choice, and a manual count or risk-limiting
audit of paper ballots is what confirms the outcome of an election, while an integrity
examination of software and logs provides assurance about the process and deters tampering but
cannot by itself prove that equipment was never altered; and

(7) a bipartisan structure without a defined method of resolving disagreement could be used to
stall the certification of an election, and any such structure must therefore resolve every
disagreement by a rule fixed in advance and within a fixed time.

(b) The purpose of this Act is to give each of the two principal political parties in a county
the right to request and observe a comprehensive reconciliation of the county's election records
and a bounded, non-destructive integrity examination of a subset of voting system equipment
before the local canvass, supervised by two co-judges appointed by the parties, while the general
custodian of election records retains custody and management authority at all times and the
canvass proceeds on its statutory schedule.

**SECTION 2.** Section 67.003, Election Code, is amended by adding Subsection (d) to read as
follows:

(d) If a pre-canvass reconciliation examination is conducted in the election under Subchapter K,
Chapter 127, the presiding officer of the local canvassing authority shall set the time for the
local canvass on a date that is not earlier than the day after the examination closing date
determined under Section 127.418. The time for the local canvass may not be set later than the
latest date permitted by Subsection (b) or (c), as applicable. The conduct of an examination does
not extend the period for the local canvass, and the failure of an examination to be completed
does not prevent or delay the canvass.

to read as follows:

(h) In an election in which a pre-canvass reconciliation examination was conducted under

> (1) the assurance statement prepared under Section 127.412;
>
> (2) the master discrepancy and resolution log prepared under Section 127.412; and

> (3) each item referred to the canvassing authority under Section 127.417.

**SECTION 4.** Chapter 127, Election Code, is amended by adding Subchapter K to read as follows:

### SUBCHAPTER K. BIPARTISAN PRE-CANVASS RECONCILIATION AND BOUNDED INTEGRITY EXAMINATION

**Sec. 127.401. DEFINITIONS.** In this subchapter:

(1) "Anomaly designation" means the designation of a unit for integrity examination by a co-judge
under Section 127.420(c).

(2) "Assurance statement" means the statement prepared under Section 127.412(c).

(3) "Co-judge" means a person appointed under Section 127.405 to supervise an examination on
behalf of an eligible political party, and includes an alternate co-judge while serving.

(4) "Dual control" means a requirement that two or more authorized persons be present for an
action, and that no person act alone with an election record, a piece of voting system equipment,
or an item of removable media.

(5) "Eligible political party" means each of the two political parties whose nominees for
governor received the highest and second highest number of votes in the county in the most recent
gubernatorial general election.

(6) "Examination" means a pre-canvass reconciliation examination conducted under this subchapter,
consisting of a records reconciliation under Section 127.410 and, if conducted, an integrity
examination under Section 127.419.

(7) "Examination closing date" means the date determined under Section 127.418.

(8) "Factual discrepancy" means a difference between two counts, totals, or records that are
required by law or by the nature of the records to agree, or a missing entry or gap in a
chain-of-custody, seal, access, or transfer record.

(9) "Forensic image" means a bit-for-bit copy of the contents of a storage medium, made using a
hardware or software write-blocking method that prevents alteration of the source, and
authenticated by a cryptographic hash value recorded before and after the copy is made.

(10) "General custodian" means the general custodian of election records for the election.

(11) "Integrity examination" means the examination of voting system equipment and removable
media described by Section 127.419.

(12) "Interpretive dispute" means a disagreement between the co-judges, or between a co-judge and
the general custodian, about the classification, cause, or significance of a factual discrepancy
or other finding, as distinguished from a disagreement about the result of a count or comparison.

(13) "Presiding co-judge" means the co-judge appointed by the county chair of the eligible
political party whose nominee for governor received the highest number of votes in the county in
the most recent gubernatorial general election.

(14) "Qualified examiner" means a person who meets the requirements of Section 127.421.

(15) "Read-only copy" means a copy of electronic election data exported from a voting system by a
method approved by the secretary of state and recorded on write-once media, or on other media
protected against modification, with a cryptographic hash value recorded at the time of export.

(16) "Reconciliation" means the comparison of two or more counts, totals, or records that relate
to the same ballots, voters, equipment, or materials, for the purpose of confirming that they
agree or identifying and documenting each factual discrepancy and its resolution.

(17) "Sensitive security information" means information described by Section 127.425(c).

(18) "Trusted build" means the version of the software and firmware of a voting system, and the
associated file manifest and cryptographic hash values, that was approved for use in this state
under Chapter 122 and that is furnished or identified by the secretary of state for verification
purposes.

(19) "Unit" means a single piece of voting system equipment, including an election management
system server or workstation, a central or precinct tabulator, a ballot-marking device, or a
direct recording electronic voting machine, and includes the removable media used with it.

**Sec. 127.402. APPLICABILITY.** (a) This subchapter applies to an election:

> (1) for which the precinct returns are canvassed by the commissioners court under
> Section 67.002(a)(1); and
>
> (2) that is conducted by the county clerk, elections administrator, or other county officer
> serving as the general custodian.

(b) This subchapter does not apply to a primary election or runoff primary election conducted
under Title 10.

(c) In a joint election, an examination under this subchapter extends only to the election
records and voting system equipment in the custody of the county officer serving as the general
custodian.

**Sec. 127.403. CUSTODY AND MANAGEMENT AUTHORITY RETAINED BY GENERAL CUSTODIAN.** (a) The general
custodian retains custody of, and ultimate management authority over the retention and security
of, every election record, ballot, item of removable media, and unit at all times during an
examination, consistent with 52 U.S.C. Section 20701 and Section 66.058 of this code.

(b) Every step of an examination shall be performed:

> (1) by the general custodian, an employee of the general custodian, an employee of the
> secretary of state assigned under Section 127.421(d), or a qualified examiner acting as the
> agent of the general custodian under a written agreement that complies with
> Section 127.421(c); and
>
> (2) in a facility controlled by the general custodian, on hardware owned or leased by and under
> the control of the general custodian or the secretary of state.

(c) A co-judge, a county chair, a political party, a candidate, a watcher, or any other person
may not take possession of, remove from the examination area, or retain a copy of an original
election record, ballot, forensic image, read-only copy, item of removable media, or unit, except
as expressly provided by this subchapter for the publication of reports.

(d) This subchapter does not authorize a political party, a co-judge, or any other person to
conduct or commission an examination of election records or voting system equipment other than
an examination conducted by or under the authority of the general custodian in accordance with
this subchapter.

**Sec. 127.404. REQUEST FOR EXAMINATION.** (a) The county chair of an eligible political party,
acting individually or as directed by the county executive committee, may request that an
examination be conducted in an election to which this subchapter applies.

(b) A request must be in writing, signed by the county chair, and delivered to the general
custodian not later than the 60th day before election day. The general custodian shall
immediately deliver a copy of the request to the county chair of the other eligible political
party and to the secretary of state.

(c) A request delivered after the deadline prescribed by Subsection (b) and not later than the
seventh day before election day is a late request. In an election for which a late request is
made, the examination includes the records reconciliation under Section 127.410 but does not
include an integrity examination under Section 127.419 unless the general custodian determines
that the integrity examination can be conducted in compliance with this subchapter.

(d) A request made by the county chair of either eligible political party entitles each eligible
political party to appoint co-judges under Section 127.405. The examination is conducted as a
single, joint examination regardless of whether one or both county chairs make a request.

(e) A county chair may make a single request that applies to each election held in the county
during a calendar year to which this subchapter applies.

(f) The general custodian may conduct an examination under this subchapter on the general
custodian's own initiative, and on doing so shall notify each county chair of an eligible
political party.

**Sec. 127.405. APPOINTMENT OF CO-JUDGES; PRESIDING CO-JUDGE.** (a) Not later than the 50th day
before election day, or not later than the third day after the date a late request is delivered,
the county chair of each eligible political party shall appoint one co-judge and one alternate
co-judge by written notice delivered to the general custodian.

(b) The co-judge appointed by the county chair of the eligible political party whose nominee for
governor received the highest number of votes in the county in the most recent gubernatorial
general election serves as the presiding co-judge.

(c) If a county chair fails to make an appointment by the deadline prescribed by Subsection (a),
or if a co-judge and alternate co-judge appointed by a county chair are both unable to serve, the
general custodian shall promptly request an appointment from the county chair. If the county
chair does not make the appointment within two days after the request, the general custodian may
appoint a qualified person who is affiliated with that political party, as shown by the person's
participation in the party's most recent primary election, and who consents to serve.

(d) The failure of a political party to have a co-judge appointed or present does not prevent,
suspend, or invalidate an examination or any part of it. A step performed while a co-judge is
absent shall be performed under dual control by employees of the general custodian, recorded
under Section 127.424, and made available for review by the absent co-judge.

(e) An alternate co-judge serves when the co-judge of the same political party is absent or
unable to serve, and has the authority of that co-judge while serving. The alternate of the
presiding co-judge serves as presiding co-judge while serving.

(f) A county chair may replace a co-judge or alternate co-judge appointed by the chair by
written notice to the general custodian. The replacement does not affect a report already signed
or a determination already made.

**Sec. 127.406. ELIGIBILITY OF CO-JUDGES.** (a) To be eligible to serve as a co-judge or
alternate co-judge, a person must:

> (1) be a qualified voter of the county;
>
> (2) meet the general eligibility requirements for an election judge under Subchapter C,
> Chapter 32, other than a requirement relating to residence in a particular election precinct;
>
> (3) complete the training prescribed under Section 127.407(c) before beginning service; and
>
> (4) execute the oath and confidentiality agreement prescribed under Section 127.407(a).

(b) A person is ineligible to serve as a co-judge or alternate co-judge if the person:

> (1) is a candidate for an office in the election;
>
> (2) is a county chair or an officer of a political party's state executive committee;
>
> (3) is an employee of the general custodian or of a vendor of voting system equipment used in
> the county, or was employed by such a vendor during the two years preceding the date of
> appointment; or
>
> (4) has been finally convicted of an offense under this code or of a felony, unless the
> person's eligibility has been restored as provided by law.

**Sec. 127.407. OATH; BOND; TRAINING; COMPENSATION.** (a) Before beginning service, a co-judge
or alternate co-judge must take an oath, in the form prescribed by the secretary of state, to
observe and report faithfully and impartially, to comply with this subchapter, and to protect
sensitive security information and confidential voter information, and must sign a
confidentiality agreement in the form prescribed by the secretary of state.

(b) A co-judge or alternate co-judge must be covered by a surety bond in the amount of $10,000,
payable to the county and conditioned on the faithful performance of the co-judge's duties and
compliance with Sections 127.403(c) and 127.425. The county shall pay the cost of the bond as an
election expense. The county may provide the coverage by a schedule or blanket bond.

(c) The secretary of state shall prescribe and make available, at no cost, a training program for
co-judges, which may be provided by the Internet, covering chain of custody, the reconciliations
required by Section 127.410, the scope and limits of an integrity examination, the resolution of
disagreements under this subchapter, and the protection of sensitive security information.

(d) A co-judge or alternate co-judge is entitled to compensation for each hour of service at the
rate paid to the presiding judge of the central counting station under Section 127.005. The
county shall pay the compensation as an election expense.

**Sec. 127.408. AUTHORITY OF CO-JUDGES.** (a) A co-judge is entitled to:

> (1) observe every step of the examination;
>
> (2) inspect, in the presence of the general custodian or the custodian's designee, any election
> record or log used in the examination;
>
> (3) ask questions of the general custodian and of any person performing a step of the
> examination and receive a response, which shall be in writing if the co-judge requests;
>
> (4) request that a step be re-performed under Section 127.413;
>
> (5) make an anomaly designation under Section 127.420(c);
>
> (6) sign or decline to sign each report and record a dissent under Section 127.416; and
>
> (7) observe the preparation of the reconciliation under Section 127.131(f), the count under
> Section 127.201, any risk-limiting audit under Subchapter I, and any test of equipment under
> Subchapter D that occurs during the examination period.

(b) A co-judge may not:

> (1) handle an original ballot, original election record, item of removable media, or unit,
> except as permitted by the general custodian under dual control;
>
> (2) operate voting system equipment or any hardware used in the examination;
>
> (3) bring into the examination area a device capable of recording, transmitting, or storing
> data, other than a device issued by the general custodian for the examination; or
>
> (4) direct the work of any person performing a step of the examination.

(c) Access by a co-judge to an election record is joint. A co-judge may not be alone with an
original ballot, original election record, item of removable media, or unit.

(d) A co-judge may view confidential voter information contained in an election record to the
extent necessary to perform the co-judge's duties but may not copy or disclose that information.

(e) A watcher who is entitled to serve at the central counting station in the election may
observe the examination, except that a watcher may not view sensitive security information.

**Sec. 127.409. EXAMINATION PLAN.** (a) Not later than the 30th day before election day, or not
later than the fifth day after the date a late request is delivered, the general custodian shall
prepare a written examination plan and deliver it to each co-judge, each county chair of an
eligible political party, and the secretary of state.

(b) The examination plan must be in the form prescribed by the secretary of state and must
include:

> (1) a schedule that places each step of the examination within the period ending on the
> examination closing date, coordinated with the processing of early voting, provisional, and
> late-arriving ballots and with the count under Section 127.201;
>
> (2) the location of the examination area and the hardware to be used;
>
> (3) the method for producing read-only copies and forensic images;
>
> (4) the video recording and logging arrangements required by Section 127.424;
>
> (5) the procedure and date for the random selection of units under Section 127.420; and
>
> (6) the names of the persons who will perform the examination and, for an integrity
> examination, of each qualified examiner.

(c) A co-judge may submit written comments on the examination plan. The general custodian shall
consider the comments and may amend the plan. The general custodian's decision on the content of
the plan is final for purposes of the examination, subject to Section 127.417(d).

**Sec. 127.410. RECORDS RECONCILIATION.** (a) The general custodian shall conduct a records
reconciliation that closes each of the following loops for each election precinct, for each
polling location, and for the county as a whole:

> (1) poll list and pollbook reconciliation: the number of voters accepted for voting at each
> polling location during early voting by personal appearance and on election day, compared with
> the number of ballots cast and tabulated for that location;
>
> (2) mail ballot accounting: the number of applications for ballots to be voted by mail
> accepted, ballots provided, ballots returned, ballots accepted, ballots rejected, ballots for
> which a defect was corrected under Chapter 87, and ballots counted, compared with the
> determinations of the signature verification committee and early voting ballot board and with
> the tabulated results;
>
> (3) provisional ballot accounting: the number of provisional ballots cast, accepted, rejected,
> and counted, compared with the records of the early voting ballot board and the voter
> registrar;
>
> (4) tabulation reconciliation: the totals reported by each tabulator compared with the totals
> in the election management system, the cast vote records, and the ballot images, and those
> totals compared with the results of the count under Section 127.201 and of any risk-limiting
> audit conducted under Subchapter I;
>
> (5) ballot accounting: the number of ballots ordered or printed, delivered to each polling
> location or to the early voting clerk, issued, spoiled, voted, cast provisionally, duplicated
> under Section 127.126, unused, and returned; and
>
> (6) chain of custody: the continuity and agreement of seal numbers, transfer records, access
> logs, and logs of the insertion and removal of removable media for each ballot box, item of
> removable media, and unit, together with the video required by Section 127.1232, the
> electronic device records required by Section 127.009, and the records of each test and hash
> validation conducted under Subchapter D or Section 129.023.

(b) The reconciliation under Section 127.131(f) is an input to the records reconciliation, and
the records reconciliation shall identify any difference between that reconciliation and the
results of the records reconciliation.

(c) If a record required to perform a reconciliation does not exist or cannot be located, the
absence of the record shall be logged as a factual discrepancy.

(d) To the extent practicable, the general custodian shall conduct the count under
Section 127.201 so that it is completed before the examination closing date. On request of the
general custodian, the secretary of state shall, to the extent practicable, prescribe dates under
Subchapter I that permit the results of a risk-limiting audit in the county to be reconciled
before the examination closing date. A comparison under Subsection (a)(4) that cannot be completed
before the examination closing date because the count or audit is not complete shall be completed
and reported under Section 127.418(d).

(e) Notwithstanding Section 66.058, a container holding voted ballots may be opened during an
examination only to conduct a count under Section 127.201, Chapter 65, or Subchapter I, and only
under dual control in the presence of the co-judges or under Section 127.405(d). A container
holding unvoted, spoiled, or other ballots that are not voted ballots may be opened for the
purpose of ballot accounting under Subsection (a)(5) under the same conditions. Each container
opened shall be resealed and the seal numbers logged.

**Sec. 127.411. READ-ONLY COPIES; CONTROLLED HARDWARE.** (a) The records reconciliation and the
review of logs shall be performed on read-only copies and forensic images, and not on original
electronic records, unless no copy can be made by a method approved by the secretary of state.

(b) Electronic data shall be exported from a voting system only by an export function of the
certified voting system or another method approved by the secretary of state, and shall be
transferred under dual control. Each person participating in the transfer shall verify and log
the files transferred and their cryptographic hash values.

(c) Hardware used to analyze a read-only copy or forensic image:

> (1) must be owned or leased by and under the control of the general custodian or the secretary
> of state;
>
> (2) may not be a component of a voting system or be connected to a component of a voting
> system; and
>
> (3) may not be connected to the Internet or to any external network during the examination.

(d) The general custodian shall record a cryptographic hash value of each original electronic
record before and after a read-only copy or forensic image is made, and shall log any difference
as a factual discrepancy.

**Sec. 127.412. RECONCILIATION REPORTS; MASTER LOG; ASSURANCE STATEMENT.** (a) The general
custodian shall prepare, for each election precinct and each polling location, a reconciliation
report in the form prescribed by the secretary of state showing the result of each loop under
Section 127.410(a) and each factual discrepancy found. Each co-judge shall sign the report, with
or without a dissent.

(b) The general custodian shall maintain a master discrepancy and resolution log that records,
for each factual discrepancy and interpretive dispute:

> (1) a unique identifier;
>
> (2) the precinct, polling location, unit, or record concerned;
>
> (3) a description of the discrepancy and the counts involved;
>
> (4) each re-performance under Section 127.413 and its result;
>
> (5) the classification under Section 127.414 and the written statement of reasons;
>
> (6) whether the item is segregated and the location and seal number of any segregated
> material;
>
> (7) any dissent; and
>
> (8) whether the item is referred under Section 127.417.

(c) On the examination closing date, the general custodian shall prepare an assurance statement
in the form prescribed by the secretary of state. The assurance statement must:

> (1) state, for each loop under Section 127.410(a), whether it was reconciled without
> discrepancy, reconciled with each discrepancy explained, or not fully reconciled;
>
> (2) list each unresolved item and each referred item;
>
> (3) summarize the results of any integrity examination completed on or before the examination
> closing date, accompanied by the statement required by Section 127.422;
>
> (4) state that the examination does not change any vote total and that a vote total may be
> corrected only as provided by Section 127.415; and
>
> (5) be signed by the general custodian and by each co-judge, with or without a dissent.

(d) A report or statement is complete when signed by the general custodian, regardless of whether
a co-judge signs it. If a co-judge declines to sign or is not present, the general custodian
shall note that fact on the document.

(e) The assurance statement and the master discrepancy and resolution log are part of the record
of the local canvass under Section 67.004(h).

**Sec. 127.413. RESOLUTION OF FACTUAL DISCREPANCY BY RE-PERFORMANCE.** (a) When a factual
discrepancy is identified, the step that produced it shall be re-performed under dual control in
the presence of the co-judges, using the same or a more direct source record, including by
recounting physical materials when the records reconciliation is authorized to do so under
Section 127.410(e).

(b) Each re-performance and its result shall be logged. When two consecutive re-performances
produce the same result, that result is established as the result of the step for purposes of
the examination.

(c) If, after three re-performances, the co-judges do not agree on the result of the step, the
disagreement is treated as an interpretive dispute and resolved under Section 127.414.

(d) Re-performance establishes the existence and amount of a factual discrepancy. It does not
determine the cause of the discrepancy, which is classified under Section 127.414.

**Sec. 127.414. RESOLUTION OF INTERPRETIVE DISPUTES.** (a) The presiding co-judge shall classify
each established factual discrepancy as:

> (1) explained, if documentation in the election records accounts for the discrepancy and the
> documented cause is permitted by law;
>
> (2) a clerical or procedural error, if the discrepancy results from an error in recording,
> transcribing, or procedure that does not indicate that a ballot was improperly cast, counted,
> or excluded; or
>
> (3) unexplained.

(b) The presiding co-judge shall resolve each other interpretive dispute that concerns the
results of the examination. The presiding co-judge has, for that purpose, authority comparable to
that of a presiding election judge, and shall give a written statement of the specific reasons
for each classification or resolution that a co-judge disputes.

(c) The general custodian shall decide each question concerning custody, security, the protection
of sensitive security information, the scope of the examination under this subchapter, and the
operation of equipment, and shall give a written statement of reasons for a decision that a
co-judge disputes. A decision under this subsection may not deny a co-judge a right granted by
Section 127.408.

(d) A question of law, including a question about the validity of a ballot or the
interpretation of this code, is not decided in the examination. The question shall be logged and
referred under Section 127.417.

(e) Any physical material that is the subject of a disputed classification or resolution shall
be placed in a separate container, sealed, labeled with the item's identifier, and logged. Any
electronic material that is the subject of a disputed classification or resolution shall be
preserved as a read-only copy with its hash value logged. Segregated material shall be preserved
under Section 127.427.

(f) A co-judge whose position does not prevail may record a dissent under Section 127.416.

**Sec. 127.415. PRESUMPTIONS; EFFECT ON OFFICIAL RESULTS.** (a) The returns and results
determined in accordance with other provisions of this code are presumed correct. The examination
does not by itself change any vote total, the acceptance or rejection of any ballot, or the
result of any election.

(b) A vote total may be corrected only by a procedure authorized by other law, including a
correction of the returns under Section 67.004(d) or 127.131, a recount under Title 13, or an
election contest under Title 14.

(c) A determination by the signature verification committee or the early voting ballot board
under Chapter 87 to accept or reject a ballot is not subject to review in the examination. The
examination reconciles the number of ballots accepted, rejected, and counted, but does not
reconsider the determination.

(d) A factual discrepancy, including a gap in a chain-of-custody record, does not by itself
invalidate a ballot or a vote total.

**Sec. 127.416. DISSENT.** (a) A co-judge may record a dissent on any reconciliation report,
entry in the master discrepancy and resolution log, or assurance statement. A dissent must be in
writing, must identify the item concerned, and must state the co-judge's reasons.

(b) A co-judge who signs a document with a dissent has signed the document for purposes of this
subchapter.

(c) A dissent does not prevent or delay the completion of a report, the examination closing
date, or the local canvass, and does not have the effect of a veto.

(d) A dissent is an election record and is preserved for use in a recount or an election contest.

**Sec. 127.417. REFERRAL TO CANVASSING AUTHORITY; JUDICIAL REVIEW.** (a) Only the following items
may be referred to the canvassing authority:

> (1) a factual discrepancy classified as unexplained;
>
> (2) an item that is the subject of a dissent; and
>
> (3) a question of law logged under Section 127.414(d).

(b) The general custodian shall deliver the referred items to the canvassing authority with the
documents required by Section 67.004(h). In acting on a referred item, the canvassing authority:

> (1) may direct a correction of the returns under Section 67.004(d) if the correction is
> otherwise authorized by law;
>
> (2) may refer the item to the secretary of state or to an appropriate prosecuting attorney; and
>
> (3) shall note the item and any action taken in the record of the canvass.

(c) The canvassing authority may not:

> (1) reopen an item that was resolved in the examination without a dissent; or
>
> (2) consider as a referred item a matter that was not referred under Subsection (a).

(d) A co-judge or the county chair of an eligible political party may seek a writ of mandamus
under Section 273.061 to compel the general custodian to perform a duty imposed by this
subchapter, including a duty to provide access under Section 127.408. A court may not order that
the local canvass be delayed beyond the latest date permitted by Section 67.003.

(e) A challenge to the result of an election based on a matter identified in an examination may
be brought only by recount under Title 13 or by election contest under Title 14.

**Sec. 127.418. DEADLINE; CANVASS NOT DELAYED.** (a) The examination closing date is the earlier
of:

> (1) the date on which the general custodian and the co-judges complete the records
> reconciliation; or
>
> (2) the second day before the latest date on which the local canvass may be conducted under
> Section 67.003(b) or (c), as applicable.

(b) At 5 p.m. on the examination closing date, the records reconciliation closes. The general
custodian shall complete the assurance statement and the master discrepancy and resolution log
on the basis of the work completed. Each item not resolved at that time is recorded as
unresolved.

(c) An item recorded as unresolved is transmitted with the record of the canvass and preserved
under Section 127.427. The existence of an unresolved item does not prevent or delay the canvass.

(d) A comparison described by Section 127.410(d) and any part of an integrity examination not
completed by the examination closing date shall be completed, and a supplemental report in the
form prescribed by the secretary of state shall be signed and published under Section 127.425,
not later than the 45th day after election day. A supplemental report does not affect the
canvass and is an election record.

**Sec. 127.419. INTEGRITY EXAMINATION.** (a) If an examination is requested, the general
custodian shall conduct an integrity examination of the units selected under Section 127.420.
Unless Subsection (c) applies, the integrity examination is limited to the following
non-destructive procedures:

> (1) verification of the cryptographic hash value of every file in the installed software and
> firmware of the unit against the file manifest of the trusted build, using a method approved
> by the secretary of state that does not alter the unit;
>
> (2) export and review of the audit logs, event logs, and system logs of the unit, including
> records of configuration changes, user access, and the insertion and removal of removable
> media, using an export function of the certified voting system or another method approved by
> the secretary of state;
>
> (3) the creation of a forensic image of each item of removable media used with the unit, and
> verification of the contents of the image against the expected contents; and
>
> (4) verification that no network interface, wireless capability, or modem of the unit is
> enabled or active, by inspection of the unit's configuration and physical ports by a method
> approved by the secretary of state.

(b) An integrity examination of a unit may not begin until the tabulation of results on that unit
is complete and the results have been preserved.

(c) An integrity examination may include opening the enclosure of a unit or making a forensic
image of internal storage only if:

> (1) the unit is selected because of a condition described by Section 127.420(d);
>
> (2) the secretary of state approves the procedure in writing; and
>
> (3) the unit is withdrawn from service until re-accepted under Section 127.423.

(d) An integrity examination may not include:

> (1) installing software on, or modifying the configuration of, a unit;
>
> (2) connecting a unit to a network;
>
> (3) review of source code, unless conducted by the secretary of state under other law;
>
> (4) penetration testing or other adversarial testing of a unit that is used or will be used in
> an election before it is re-accepted under Section 127.423; or
>
> (5) any procedure that destroys or alters a ballot, election data, or log.

**Sec. 127.420. SELECTION OF UNITS.** (a) Not later than the second day after election day, at
a public drawing held by the general custodian and observed by the co-judges, the general
custodian shall select units for integrity examination at random by a method prescribed by the
secretary of state that uses a publicly verifiable source of randomness. The drawing shall
select:

> (1) each election management system server and workstation used to tabulate or report results
> in the election;
>
> (2) each central tabulator used in the election;
>
> (3) the greater of two or three percent of the precinct tabulators used in the election,
> rounded up to the nearest whole number; and
>
> (4) the greater of two or one percent of the ballot-marking devices and direct recording
> electronic voting machines used in the election, rounded up to the nearest whole number.

(b) The secretary of state by rule may set a maximum number of units selected under
Subsection (a)(3) or (4) for a county, based on the number of units the county uses, if the
maximum is sufficient to permit the statement required by Section 127.422(b).

(c) In addition to the units selected at random, each co-judge may make an anomaly designation of
not more than two units, accompanied by a written statement of the basis for the designation. A
unit designated by a co-judge shall be examined under Section 127.419(a), and the designation is
not subject to the approval of the other co-judge or the general custodian.

(d) A unit shall be added to the units examined, and may be examined under Section 127.419(c),
if:

> (1) the unit is associated with a factual discrepancy classified as unexplained that exceeds a
> threshold prescribed by the secretary of state;
>
> (2) the verification under Section 127.419(a)(1) shows a hash value that does not match the
> trusted build;
>
> (3) a log reviewed under Section 127.419(a)(2) shows the insertion of unexpected removable
> media, a change in configuration after the most recent test under Subchapter D or
> Section 129.023, or network activity; or
>
> (4) a seal on the unit is missing, broken, or has a number that does not match the record.

(e) To the extent consistent with Subsection (a), the general custodian shall schedule an
integrity examination under Section 127.419(c) for a unit that is not needed for an election
held before the unit can be re-accepted under Section 127.423.

**Sec. 127.421. QUALIFIED EXAMINERS.** (a) An integrity examination shall be performed by a
qualified examiner and observed by the co-judges under dual control.

(b) A qualified examiner must:

> (1) hold a certification in digital forensics recognized by the secretary of state, or a
> license in digital and multimedia evidence analysis issued by the Texas Forensic Science
> Commission;
>
> (2) complete training prescribed by the secretary of state on the voting system used in the
> county;
>
> (3) submit to a criminal history record check; and
>
> (4) not be a candidate in the election, an officer of a political party, or a person who has,
> during the two years preceding the examination, been compensated by a candidate, political
> party, political committee, or vendor of voting system equipment used in the county.

(c) A qualified examiner who is not an employee of the general custodian or of the secretary of
state may perform an integrity examination only as the agent of the general custodian under a
written agreement that:

> (1) provides that the general custodian retains custody and control of every record, image,
> and unit;
>
> (2) prohibits the examiner from removing, retaining, or transmitting any record, image, or
> data, or using any hardware other than hardware described by Section 127.411(c);
>
> (3) requires the examiner to be bonded and to protect sensitive security information under
> Section 127.425; and
>
> (4) requires the delivery to the general custodian of all work product and the destruction of
> any working copy at the conclusion of the engagement.

(d) The secretary of state shall maintain the capacity to provide qualified examiners, through
employees of the secretary of state or through contracts under which the examiners act as agents
of the general custodian, and shall make that capacity available to a county on request. The
secretary of state may not charge a county for a qualified examiner provided under this
subsection.

(e) An employee of a vendor of voting system equipment may assist a qualified examiner in
operating a vendor tool approved by the secretary of state, under the direct supervision of the
qualified examiner and dual control, but may not serve as the qualified examiner.

**Sec. 127.422. LIMITS OF INTEGRITY EXAMINATION; REQUIRED STATEMENT.** (a) Each report of an
integrity examination must include the following statement: "This examination compared the
software and firmware installed on the examined equipment with the version approved for use in
Texas, reviewed the equipment's logs and removable media, and checked for network connections.
It can detect unexpected files, mismatched software, and logged events that indicate tampering or
error. It cannot prove that equipment was never altered, and it applies only to the equipment
examined. The paper ballots, as confirmed by manual count and audit, are the record of the
voters' choices."

(b) Each report of an integrity examination must also state, using a method prescribed by the
secretary of state, the probability that the units selected at random under
Section 127.420(a)(3) and (4) would include at least one affected unit if a stated percentage of
those units were affected.

**Sec. 127.423. RE-ACCEPTANCE OF EQUIPMENT.** (a) A unit whose enclosure is opened, whose internal
storage is imaged, or whose seal is broken during an integrity examination may not be used in an
election until:

> (1) the software and firmware of the unit are verified against, or reinstalled from, the
> trusted build by a method approved by the secretary of state;
>
> (2) the unit passes a hash validation in the manner required by Section 129.023 and a test
> under Subchapter D, as applicable;
>
> (3) the unit is resealed, with seal numbers logged, in the presence of the co-judges if they are
> serving, or otherwise of the testing board; and
>
> (4) the unit meets any additional requirement prescribed by the secretary of state to ensure
> that the unit conforms to the voting system approved under Chapter 122.

(b) A seal broken to perform a procedure under Section 127.419(a) shall be replaced and the new
seal number logged under dual control. The secretary of state by rule shall determine which
procedures under Section 127.419(a) also require compliance with Subsection (a).

(c) The performance of an integrity examination in compliance with this subchapter does not by
itself affect the approval of a voting system under Chapter 122.

**Sec. 127.424. CHAIN-OF-CUSTODY AND DUAL-CONTROL SAFEGUARDS.** (a) Each step of an examination
shall be performed under dual control.

(b) The general custodian shall maintain a continuous log of the examination that records, for
each step, the date and time, the persons present, the records or units involved, the
cryptographic hash values of electronic files used or produced, and the seal numbers removed and
applied.

(c) The general custodian shall record the examination area by video during each step of the
examination, if feasible. In a county to which Section 127.1232(b) applies, the video shall be
made available to the public by livestream in the manner provided by that section. A camera
shall be positioned so that it does not capture sensitive security information.

(d) Tamper-evident seals shall be applied to each container, item of removable media, and unit
when not in use, and each seal removed or applied shall be logged.

(e) Removable media that has been connected to a device other than a component of the voting
system shall be scanned on a standalone device designated by the general custodian before it is
connected to any hardware used in the examination.

(f) The secretary of state shall prescribe a procedure, to be followed in an examination, for
preserving evidence and maintaining custody when a finding indicates possible tampering.

**Sec. 127.425. PUBLIC REPORTS; SENSITIVE SECURITY INFORMATION.** (a) Not later than the third
day after the date of the local canvass, the general custodian shall post on the county's
Internet website, with the election returns and the reconciliation required by Section 127.131(f),
each reconciliation report, the master discrepancy and resolution log, the assurance statement,
each dissent, and a summary of each integrity examination completed. The general custodian shall
post each supplemental report not later than the third day after it is signed.

(b) Before posting a document under Subsection (a), the general custodian shall redact sensitive
security information and information that is confidential under other law, and shall describe
the category of each redaction.

(c) The following information obtained or produced in an examination is sensitive security
information:

> (1) passwords, encryption keys, and other authentication credentials;
>
> (2) details of the configuration of a unit or system that would assist a person in gaining
> unauthorized access to or altering the unit or system;
>
> (3) the content of a trusted build file manifest or reference file that the secretary of state
> has furnished in confidence; and
>
> (4) other information that the secretary of state by rule determines would, if disclosed,
> create a material risk to the security of a voting system.

(d) Sensitive security information is confidential and is not subject to disclosure under
Chapter 552, Government Code, and is information described by Section 552.139, Government Code.

(e) The fact that information is redacted under this section does not prevent the disclosure of
the existence, nature, and resolution of a factual discrepancy or finding.

**Sec. 127.426. ANALYSIS SOFTWARE.** (a) Software used in an examination solely to analyze
read-only copies or forensic images is not a voting system or voting system equipment for
purposes of Chapter 122 if:

> (1) the software is not installed on and does not connect to a component of a voting system;
>
> (2) the software is operated on hardware described by Section 127.411(c);
>
> (3) the software opens its input files in a read-only mode and does not write to an official
> election record;
>
> (4) the software records in a log each input file and its cryptographic hash value, each
> parameter used, and each output produced; and
>
> (5) the findings produced by the software are used only to inform the examination and the
> canvassing authority, and are not used to determine a vote total or whether a ballot is
> accepted.

(b) The secretary of state may prescribe functional acceptance criteria for software used under
this section, including criteria for correctness, detection of discrepancies, determinism,
read-only operation, logging, and the publication of a cryptographic hash value for each version,
and may publish a list of software that has been shown to meet those criteria.

(c) The secretary of state may prescribe formats for the data used and the reports produced in an
examination, and shall, to the extent practicable, use published common data formats for
election results and cast vote records.

**Sec. 127.427. PRESERVATION OF EXAMINATION RECORDS; USE IN RECOUNT AND CONTEST.** (a) Each
report, log, dissent, statement, video recording, read-only copy, forensic image, and item of
segregated material produced in an examination is an election record and shall be preserved by
the general custodian for the period for preserving the precinct election records of the election.

(b) A record described by Subsection (a) may be considered by a recount committee under
Title 13 and by a court in an election contest under Title 14.

(c) A read-only copy or forensic image shall be preserved on media in the custody of the general
custodian. The general custodian may not release a copy of a forensic image of a unit except to
the secretary of state, to a court, or as otherwise required by law.

**Sec. 127.428. OFFENSES.** (a) A person commits an offense if the person knowingly discloses
sensitive security information obtained through an examination to a person who is not authorized
by this subchapter or other law to receive it. An offense under this subsection is a Class A
misdemeanor, except that the offense is a state jail felony if the person discloses the
information with the intent to facilitate unauthorized access to or alteration of a voting
system.

(b) A person commits an offense if, without the authorization of the general custodian, the
person knowingly:

> (1) removes from the examination area, or retains, an original election record, ballot,
> read-only copy, forensic image, item of removable media, or unit; or
>
> (2) connects a device to a unit or to hardware used in the examination.

(c) An offense under Subsection (b) is a state jail felony.

(d) If conduct that constitutes an offense under this section also constitutes an offense under
another law, the actor may be prosecuted under this section, the other law, or both.

**Sec. 127.429. RULES; FORMS.** (a) The secretary of state shall adopt rules and prescribe forms
to implement this subchapter, including:

> (1) the forms for the request, appointment, oath, confidentiality agreement, examination plan,
> reconciliation reports, master discrepancy and resolution log, assurance statement, dissent, and
> supplemental report, each of which shall be available in a machine-readable format;
>
> (2) guidance for each voting system approved for use in this state on verifying installed
> software and firmware against the trusted build, exporting logs, and verifying the disabling of
> network capabilities;
>
> (3) the random selection method and the probability method required by Sections 127.420 and
> 127.422;
>
> (4) the thresholds required by Section 127.420(d)(1);
>
> (5) the training required by Sections 127.407 and 127.421;
>
> (6) the categories of sensitive security information under Section 127.425(c)(4); and
>
> (7) the re-acceptance procedures required by Section 127.423.

(b) As a condition of the approval of a voting system under Chapter 122, the secretary of state
may require the vendor to furnish the trusted build file manifest and hash values and the
documentation and tools necessary to export logs and verify software and firmware without
altering the equipment.

**Sec. 127.430. REPORT TO LEGISLATURE.** Not later than January 15 of each odd-numbered year, the
secretary of state shall submit to the governor, the lieutenant governor, the speaker of the house
of representatives, and the standing committees of the legislature with jurisdiction over
elections a report on examinations conducted under this subchapter during the preceding two
years, including the number of examinations requested, the costs incurred by counties and the
state, a summary of factual discrepancies by category, the results of integrity examinations,
the number of referred and unresolved items, and recommendations for legislative or
administrative changes.

**Sec. 127.431. CONSTRUCTION.** This subchapter does not limit:

> (1) the rights of a watcher under Chapter 33;
>
> (2) a recount under Title 13 or an election contest under Title 14;
>
> (3) the count required by Section 127.201, a risk-limiting audit under Subchapter I, or an
> audit under Subchapter J; or
>
> (4) the authority of the secretary of state under other law.

**SECTION 5.** (a) Not later than December 1, 2027, the secretary of state shall adopt the rules
and prescribe the forms required by Subchapter K, Chapter 127, Election Code, as added by this
Act.

(b) Not later than September 1, 2028, the secretary of state shall establish the capacity
required by Section 127.421(d), Election Code, as added by this Act.

(c) The secretary of state shall submit the first report required by Section 127.430, Election
Code, as added by this Act, not later than January 15, 2029.

**SECTION 6.** The changes in law made by this Act apply only to an election held on or after
January 1, 2028. An election held before January 1, 2028, is governed by the law in effect when
the election was ordered, and that law is continued in effect for that purpose.

**SECTION 7.** If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is
held invalid, the invalidity does not affect other provisions or applications of this Act that
can be given effect without the invalid provision or application, and to this end the provisions
of this Act are declared to be severable.

**SECTION 8.** To the extent of any conflict, this Act prevails over another Act of the 90th
Legislature, Regular Session, 2027, relating to nonsubstantive additions to and corrections in
enacted codes.

**SECTION 9.** This Act takes effect September 1, 2027.

## Notes for the Legislative Council (not part of the bill)

1. **Subchapter letter and section numbers.** The 2025 edition of Chapter 127 ends with
   Subchapter J (Randomized Audits, Section 127.351). This draft adds Subchapter K beginning at
   Section 127.401. Please confirm that no 2027 bill claims the same numbers.
2. **Section 67.003.** The current section has Subsections (b) and (c) (no Subsection (a)
   appears in the current text). New Subsection (d) is added after (c).
3. **Section 67.004.** The current section has Subsections (a) through (g) as amended by
   S.B. 2753, 89th Legislature (2025). New Subsections (h) through (j) follow.
4. **Section 66.058.** Rather than inserting a new subsection with a letter that could not be
   verified, the draft uses a "notwithstanding Section 66.058" clause in Section 127.410(e).
   The Council may prefer a direct amendment to Section 66.058.
5. **Section 127.005 alternate.** Section 127.005 requires an alternate presiding judge at the
   central counting station only in elections in which judges are appointed under
   Section 32.002. Section 1(a)(2)(A) of this draft describes that pairing in those terms.
6. **Chapter 122 cross-references.** Section 127.423(a)(4) and Section 127.429(b) refer to
   approval under Chapter 122 generally. Please conform them to the specific approval and
   reexamination provisions if a narrower citation is preferred.
7. **Emergency clause.** The draft uses a September 1, 2027 effective date, which needs no
   supermajority. If the sponsor wants the rules and examiner capacity to be in place earlier, an
   immediate-effect clause can replace Section 9, and the dates in Sections 5 and 6 can be moved
   up.
8. **Repealer.** No provision of existing law is repealed. Conflicts are handled by the specific
   "notwithstanding" clause in Section 127.410(e) and by Section 8.
