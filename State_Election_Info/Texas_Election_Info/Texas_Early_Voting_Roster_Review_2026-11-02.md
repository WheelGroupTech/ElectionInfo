# Texas Early Voting Rosters: County and State Data Review

**Election:** November 3, 2026 General Election\
**Data through:** Friday, October 30, 2026 (twelfth and last day of in-person early voting)\
**Prepared:** November 2, 2026 (updates the reviews of October 20-31; county files as posted by 10 PM Sunday, November 1; state files as re-downloaded on the evening of November 1 and checked again on the morning of November 2)\
**Scope:** the 50 most populous Texas counties and the Texas Secretary of State (SOS) roster portal

## 1. Summary

Texas Election Code §87.121 requires each county to post its early voting rosters (in-person and by mail) daily and to submit them for statewide elections to the Secretary of State, which republishes them. This update compares the county and state lists voter by voter (by Voter Unique Identifier, VUID) for all twelve days of in-person early voting, October 19-30, in the 50 most populous counties.

Key findings:

1. **The two sources agree closely, but not completely, in every county.** Over every day for which both are complete, the county's and the state's lists of early in-person voters agree on 99.0% or more of voters in 30 of the 33 counties with dated county rosters, and on 98.9% in Fort Bend and Hunt and 98.6% in Guadalupe. In none of them do the two lists match exactly: every county has at least one voter on one list and not the other. (In this review a 100% figure is shown only when no voter is on one list and not the other.) Sixteen voters are an in-person voter in one source and a mail voter in the other (Hunt 11, Collin 2, and one each in Dallas, Hidalgo and Cameron).
2. **The state's files are now complete for all twelve days.** Harris's October 30 file, about half complete two days ago, now holds 126,294 of the county's 126,397 voters, and Tarrant's 62,214 of 62,629; every county's October 30 file holds at least 98.5% of the county's count (Fort Bend 31,557 of 32,032). With October 30 counted, the county and state lists agree on 99.90% of Harris's and 99.3% of Tarrant's in-person voters. The statewide turnout table now shows 844,468 in-person voters for October 30, the most of any day (7,456,336 for October 19-30).
3. **The state keeps revising earlier days, and removed a quarter of Ellis's mail voters.** Since the October 31 review, 74 county-day counts for October 14-30 changed in the state's files. The 22 in-person counts are net +98,753, almost all of it Harris's and Tarrant's October 30 files filled in (+67,593 and +30,517; Dallas's +581). The 52 mail counts are net +13 (29 of them downward), but Ellis's mail voters fell from 1,230 to 878: its files for October 19-26 lost almost all of theirs (October 23 from 63 to 2, October 21 from 52 to 0) and its October 27 fell from 292 to 221. Cameron's October 29 mail rose from 424 to 556 while its October 21 fell from 113 to 66 and its October 26 from 137 to 94. The turnout table's statewide mail total fell from 259,915 to 259,664.
4. **Twenty voters are recorded as voting in two counties,** unchanged since the October 31 review. The three added then voted in person in one county on October 23 (Bexar, Travis, Brazos) and in another on October 30 (Harris, Bexar, Harris), with the same first and last name in both counties. The state lists each only in the county where the voter first voted; its completed Harris October 30 file does not list the two Harris votes (§6.8).
5. **Forty voters have both a mail ballot and an in-person vote in the same county,** unchanged since the October 31 review. The six added then all voted in person on October 30: one each in Brazoria (mail received September 29), Dallas (October 27), Denton (October 14), El Paso (October 13), Galveston (October 30) and Jefferson (on its mail list through October 30). The state lists only the in-person vote for all six (§6.9).
6. **Galveston's October 30 check-in list gives ten voters another voter's VUID.** Ten consecutive rows (7038-7047) carry the VUIDs of ten other voters who checked in the same day further down the list, with different names and precincts. The state's October 30 Galveston file holds none of the ten VUIDs (§6.7).
7. **The turnout table still overstates Travis, Ellis, Hays, Coryell and Henderson, and Orange, Potter and Smith for October 29.** For October 23 the portal's table shows 50,272 Travis in-person voters, against 40,486 in the state's own Travis file and 40,453 in Travis County's roster. Its October 30 figures now agree with the state's files.
8. **Mail lists that trail the state's.** Collin's mail list posted October 30 lacks 599 state mail voters (402 dated October 30 by the state, 107 October 29), and Dallas's, also posted October 30, lacks 277 (83 dated October 30). Galveston has posted no mail list for October 27 or 29, and lacks 324 state mail voters (218 dated October 29, 91 October 27). Wichita's latest list runs through October 29 and lacks 169 (105 dated October 29), Grayson's runs through October 29 and lacks 61 (59 dated October 30), and Tom Green's runs through October 28 and lacks 66. Cameron's list has caught up (20 missing).
9. **Five counties had posted nothing for October 30 by the night of November 1:** Grayson, Henderson and Angelina (in-person), Tom Green (nothing since its October 28 roster) and Guadalupe (no in-person roster since October 27).
10. **New file names.** Dallas renamed its in-person file again ("In-Person-Early-Voting-Details-Report_10.30.26.csv") and added a turnout-by-location workbook; Collin's in-person list is again named "early-voting-through-..."; Jefferson's in-person list dropped "Voting" from its name ("JeffersonCountyInPerson10302026.xls"); Fort Bend's files gained a "_1" suffix; Bexar's October 30 check-in list is again named for 2028 ("10302028"). All load without change to the loader. On November 1 only Harris (mail) and El Paso (mail through October 31) posted new lists.
11. **Williamson's mail precincts are still wrong.** Its latest file (posted October 31) gives a precinct different from the state's for 4,685 mail voters (and three in-person voters). **Bowie's October 20, 21 and 23 reports** disagree with the state on the precinct of 4,717 voters; **Brazos's mail precincts** differ for 163 voters.
12. **The state's county files contain voters of other counties.** Across October 14-30, 2,908 voters (2,870 in-person, 38 mail) appear in an SOS county file while being on a different county's own roster, with that county's precinct.
13. **The state's files continue to name voters that Tarrant County redacts.** Tarrant's cumulative in-person list held 50 redacted records on October 31 (the list through October 30), down from 51; its cumulative mail list held 168, down from 169.
14. **Records that warrant review by the counties** (none is proof of wrongdoing; most have clerical explanations):
    - 40 voters listed both as a returned mail ballot and as an early in-person voter in the same county (Dallas 16, El Paso 8, Jefferson 4, Grayson 3, Tarrant 2, Tom Green 2, Travis 2, and one each in Brazoria, Denton and Galveston);
    - 74 VUIDs that name entirely different people in two records, 13 of them within Galveston's own files and 12 on Bell's roster;
    - 191 same-day duplicate check-ins in 12 counties (154 of them in Angelina), and 32 mail ballots listed as received on two days (15 in Bexar);
    - precinct differences besides Williamson, Bowie and Brazos: Harris 3,501, Tarrant 303, El Paso 253, Galveston 186, Smith 115, Bexar 70.

## 2. Scope and method

**Counties (by 2025 Census population):**

- Ranks 1-20: Harris, Dallas, Tarrant, Bexar, Travis, Collin, Denton, Fort Bend, Hidalgo, El Paso, Montgomery, Williamson, Cameron, Brazoria, Bell, Galveston, Nueces, Lubbock, Hays, Webb.
- Ranks 21-50: McLennan, Jefferson, Smith, Brazos, Ellis, Johnson, Kaufman, Comal, Guadalupe, Midland, Parker, Ector, Grayson, Randall, Taylor, Rockwall, Wichita, Gregg, Hunt, Liberty, Tom Green, Bastrop, Potter, Bowie, Victoria, Henderson, Angelina, Orange, Coryell, Walker.

**Sources:**

- each county's own roster files, downloaded daily October 20 - November 1. 34 counties post their own rosters, and all 34 are compared in the tables below (Guadalupe's mail lists are scanned images, so only its in-person rosters are compared). The other 16 (Nueces, Lubbock, Hays, Ellis, Johnson, Kaufman, Comal, Midland, Parker, Ector, Taylor, Rockwall, Liberty, Orange, Coryell, Walker) link to the SOS portal;
- the SOS portal's per-county early voting CSVs for October 14-30, for all 50 counties, all days re-downloaded on the evening of November 1, since the state revises them (a second complete pass that night and a third on the morning of November 2 found no further change);
- the SOS turnout tables for October 19-30.

**Method.** All files were read with Election Explorer's roster loader, which normalizes each county's layout to VUID, precinct, name, voting method and date. For every county file, the number of VUIDs loaded matches an independent count of the 10-digit identifiers in the raw file exactly for 454 of the 465 files read. Six of the other eleven are the corrections listed below (rows dated after October 30 and Tom Green's partial day, left out on purpose). Of the remaining five: Dallas's in-person CSV and El Paso's October 30 in-person file each hold one 10-digit number in an address, not a voter; one Travis mail workbook differs by one identifier, as in earlier reviews; in Guadalupe's October 26 roster the loader finds 14 VUIDs the independent count misses (the PDF prints them run together with the ZIP code); and in its October 27 roster, the loader keeps 20 rows without a VUID, and the independent count finds 12 more VUIDs than the loader reads. No loader update was needed for this review. Webb's October 28 mail list gives no VUIDs; its voters were matched by name to the state's Webb mail voters (exact last and first name, or the same last name with one first name the start of the other), which assigned VUIDs to 47 of its 52 rows. Corrections made before comparing: mail ballots the county lists as received October 31, after the period of this review, are left out (Harris 1,439, Tarrant 818, Williamson 305, El Paso 267, Galveston 4); Tom Green's latest roster (posted October 29, named "102826") holds 330 records of October 29, part of that day, which are left out. Angelina's lists were taken from its cumulative October 25 file and its October 26-29 files. The lists were then compared by VUID:

- *early in-person, by day*: the county's in-person voters for each of October 19-30 vs. the SOS's IN-PERSON voters for that date. For counties whose lists are cumulative and undated (Tarrant, Hidalgo, Denton, El Paso, Potter), each day was recovered from consecutive daily snapshots (a day's voters = that day's snapshot minus the previous day's). A day counts toward the totals only when both sources have it and the state's file holds at least 90% of the county's count;
- *mail*: the county's latest mail list (or all daily files) vs. the SOS's MAIL-IN voters for October 14-30.

"County-only" and "state-only" voters were checked against the other 49 counties' files. Name and precinct comparisons ignore formatting (case, spacing, leading zeros, "Precinct"/"P"/"S" prefixes, and a two-digit precinct split such as "418603" for precinct 4186). Voter names and VUIDs are deliberately not reproduced in this report; the companion supplement lists the VUIDs and file locations of the anomalies for county and state staff.

## 3. The Secretary of State portal

**Where:** "Election Information & Turnout Data", `https://goelect.txelections.civixapps.com/ivis-evr-ui/evr`. Pick an election and an early voting date, then *Submit*: a table of registered voters, in-person and mail turnout per county, with a **CSV Download** per county under *Voter Details Report*.

**How the data is served.** The page fetches JSON whose `upload` field is the file in base64:

| Data | Request |
|---|---|
| Elections, early voting dates and county ids | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFile?type=EVR_ELECTION` |
| Turnout table for one date | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFile?type=EVR_EARLYVOTING&electionId=53815&electionDate=10/30/2026` |
| One county's voter CSV for one date | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFileByFormat?type=EVR_EARLYVOTING&electionId=53815&countyId=<id>&county=<NAME>&electionDate=<MM/DD/YYYY>&format=CSV` |

County ids follow alphabetical order, except that the portal sorts McLennan after McCulloch (id 161). An empty response means the county reported nothing for that day.

**File contents.** `VOTER_NAME` ("LAST, FIRST MIDDLE"), `ID_VOTER` (VUID), `VOTING_METHOD` (`IN-PERSON` / `MAIL-IN`), `PRECINCT`. There is **no address and no date column**. A county CSV holds only the voters credited on that date.

**Timeliness and revisions.** A day's files fill in over the following day or two. By the evening of November 1, all 50 counties had a complete in-person file for each of October 19-30: Harris's October 30 file, with 58,701 of the county's 126,397 voters on October 31, now holds 126,294, and Tarrant's 62,214 of 62,629 (31,697 on October 31); every county's October 30 file holds at least 98.5% (Fort Bend 31,557 of 32,032). The only other partial files are Gregg's October 19 and Hunt's October 20, partial since the first week. Nueces's October 30 file holds 9,456 voters; its October 25 and 27 files remain small (534 and 1,889). Re-downloading every day for every county on the evening of November 1 changed 51 files from the download earlier that day. Compared with the files used in the October 31 review, 74 county-day counts for October 14-30 differ: 22 in-person counts (net +98,753, of which Harris's October 30 file +67,593, Tarrant's +30,517 and Dallas's +581; none downward) and 52 mail counts (net +13 voters, 29 of them downward). The state re-dates and removes mail ballots as well as adding them: Ellis's mail voters fell from 1,230 to 878, its October 19-26 files losing almost all of theirs (October 19 from 40 to 0, October 21 from 52 to 0, October 23 from 63 to 2, October 24 from 60 to 1) and its October 27 falling from 292 to 221; Cameron's October 29 rose from 424 to 556 while its October 21 fell from 113 to 66 and its October 26 from 137 to 94; Hays's October 28 rose from 55 to 94 while its October 27 fell from 19 to 1; Tarrant's October 30 mail rose from 399 to 588 and Dallas's from 530 to 615. Over the two reviews before this one the state had changed 177 more county-day counts for October 14-29, including Johnson's October 26 mail (206 to 824) and Harris's October 29 in-person file (61,712 to 88,916).

**Mail dates.** The date of an SOS mail file is not the day the county received the ballot, and the state moves ballots from one day's file to another. In Travis, the state's October 16 mail voters are almost all ballots the county lists as received October 13, and its October 19 mail voters mostly the county's October 14 ballots. Mail comparisons in this review are therefore made over all days together.

**Turnout table vs. CSVs.** The statewide table now shows 784,600 in-person voters for October 19, 745,514 for October 20, 684,258 for October 21, 633,713 for October 22, 701,242 for October 23, 518,904 for October 24, 268,459 for October 25, 584,110 for October 26, 551,199 for October 27, 549,465 for October 28, 590,404 for October 29 and 844,468 for October 30 (7,456,336 in all), and 259,664 mail voters (259,915 on October 31). It agrees with the state's CSVs for every county and day except Travis (October 23: table 50,272, CSV 40,486), Ellis (October 23: 8,108 vs. 6,326), Hays (October 22: 10,861 vs. 8,791), Coryell (October 20: 1,867 vs. 1,442), Henderson (October 25: 513 vs. 441), for October 29 Orange (1,602 vs. 1,288), Potter (1,912 vs. 1,560) and Smith (5,130 vs. 4,960), whose own rosters show 1,556 and 4,994 for Potter and Smith. The October 30 table, which understated Tarrant on October 31, now agrees with the CSVs.

**Name formatting.** The SOS appends name suffixes to the middle name without a space ("RAYJR"), which causes apparent name mismatches against county files.

## 6. Anomalies between county and state data

### 6.1 State county files include voters of other counties

Across October 14-30, 2,908 voters (2,870 in-person, 38 mail) in the SOS county files appear on a different county's own roster, with the same first name and that county's precinct. The largest numbers are in the SOS files for Travis (311), Dallas (266), Bexar (239), Denton (234), Collin (151), Johnson (137), Hays (129), Harris (125), Tarrant (115) and Brazos (107). No voter appears in two SOS county files, so these are records assigned to the wrong county's file, not duplicates. Anyone using SOS county files alone will slightly overcount each county.

### 6.2 State files identify voters Tarrant County redacts

Tarrant replaces the name, VUID and address of protected voters with "--Redacted--", keeping only precinct and date: 211 records in its current files through October 30 (161 mail, 50 early in-person). No other county redacts identities; Harris hides the address (only) of named voters.

- **Mail:** the SOS Tarrant mail files list 149 mail voters who are not on Tarrant's mail roster (148 after excluding one voter of another county). For 118 of them there is a redacted Tarrant mail record with the same precinct and the same date.
- **In person:** the SOS Tarrant files list 97 in-person voters (besides 114 of other counties) who are not on Tarrant's roster on any day; 49 of them match a redacted Tarrant in-person record by precinct.
- **Redacted counts:** Tarrant's cumulative in-person list held 104 redacted records on October 20, falling each day to 47 in the afternoon file of October 27, then 53 in the evening file of October 27 and on October 29, 51 on October 30 and 50 on October 31 (the list through October 30); its cumulative mail list held 215 in the October 23 afternoon file, 202 in the evening file, 192 on October 25, 186 on October 27, 182 in the October 27 evening file, 175 on October 29, 169 on October 30 and 168 on October 31 (7 of them received October 31). A cumulative list should not shrink.

With twelve days of data, the match remains close for both voting methods. This continues to indicate that the state publishes the name, VUID and precinct of voters whose identities Tarrant withholds. It cannot be proven record by record, because the redacted rows carry no identifier. Neither source exposes an address.

### 6.3 Reporting gaps in the state data

- **In-person:** every county has a state file for every day of October 19-30, and Harris's and Tarrant's October 30 files are now complete (126,294 of 126,397 and 62,214 of 62,629). ## 8. Recommendations

1. **Download daily** through Election Day: Tarrant, Hidalgo, Denton, El Paso and Potter (no voting date per voter), Victoria, Tom Green, Jefferson and McLennan (cumulative files replaced), and Collin, Williamson, Cameron, Dallas, Fort Bend and Harris (latest file only). Save same-named files with the download time. Mail lists keep growing through Election Day.
2. **Prefer county files** for counts and voter lists; use the SOS portal for the 16 counties that post nothing else and as a cross-check. Re-download every SOS day before each comparison, since the state fills in, revises and re-dates them, and do not read SOS mail dates as received dates or the turnout table as a substitute for the county CSVs.
3. **When using SOS county files,** expect voters of other counties in each file (§6.1) and check them against neighboring counties before treating them as anomalies.
4. **Refer to the counties:**
   - the twenty voters recorded in two counties (§6.8), first the three who voted again on October 30;
   - the 40 voters with both a mail ballot and an in-person vote, first the six of October 30;
   - the shared VUIDs (§6.7), first those on Bell's roster and the ten rows of Galveston's October 30 check-in list;
   - Williamson's mail precincts since October 22, Bowie's October 20, 21 and 23 precincts and Brazos's mail precincts;
   - Webb's October 28 mail list without VUIDs (5 of its voters have no state mail voter of that name);
   - Angelina's repeated check-ins;
   - Hunt's mail ballots in its in-person lists;
   - Bexar's mail ballots received twice;
   - the precinct differences in Harris, Tarrant, El Paso, Galveston, Smith and Bexar;
   - the mail lists that trail the state's (Collin, Galveston, Wichita, Gregg, Tom Green, Grayson) and the missing October 30 rosters (Grayson, Henderson, Angelina, Tom Green, Guadalupe).
5. **Refer to Tarrant County and the Secretary of State:**
   - the publication by the SOS of voters Tarrant redacts, and the changing counts of redacted records (§6.2);
   - the SOS files' inclusion of other counties' voters (§6.1), including the votes filed under a second county in §6.8;
   - the re-dating and removal of mail ballots in earlier daily files, including Ellis's (§3);
   - the turnout table's Travis, Ellis, Hays, Coryell, Henderson, Orange, Potter and Smith figures (§3).
6. **Repeat this comparison** after Election Day and after the canvass.

## Appendix A. Files

Downloaded files are saved per county:

- county files are saved as `G26_<county file name>`; files a county posts under the same name every day get the download date (`_2026-10-31`), or the download date and time (`_2026-10-31-22-19-57`) for cumulative lists that carry no voting dates (Hidalgo, Potter);
- SOS files are saved as `G26_<COUNTY>.2026 NOVEMBER GENERAL ELECTION.EarlyVoting.<MM_DD_YYYY>.csv`; when the state revises a day, the earlier copy is kept.

The files load in Election Explorer (File → Load Voter Roster); the comparisons in this report were produced from the loader's normalized output. The companion supplement (`Texas_Early_Voting_Roster_Review_2026-11-02_Supplement`) lists each anomalous record's VUID and its location in the county's and the state's files.

## Appendix B. Caveats

- In-person comparisons cover October 19-30; where the state's file for a day is partial or missing, that day is shown but left out of the totals.
- County files were downloaded October 20 - November 1 at different times; several counties post same-day lists that are later replaced, and counties keep revising earlier days. The state files were re-downloaded on the evening of November 1, about an hour after the county files, and checked again on the morning of November 2.
- Day-level counts for cumulative, undated county lists were recovered from consecutive snapshots and attribute voters added late to the day they first appear (Tarrant, §5.1).
- A match between two records means the same VUID; name and precinct differences were checked separately. A match by name, precinct or date without a common VUID (§2, §6.2, §6.11) is an inference, not a confirmed identity.
- This review describes data as published. It does not establish that any voter voted improperly; the records flagged in §6.5-§6.10 have ordinary clerical explanations as well as serious ones, and only the counties can resolve them.
