# Bipartisan Pre-Canvass Reconciliation Act: Bill Summary and Talking Points

*For legislators, county party chairs, and election administrators. 90th Legislature, 2027.*

## What it does, in one sentence

At the request of either major party's county chair, the county's election office reconciles every
count against every other count before the canvass, with one co-judge from each party watching and
signing, and checks a sample of voting equipment against its approved software. Nothing leaves the
election office's custody, and certification stays on schedule.

## The problem

- Texans of both parties want to see the numbers add up, and they want to see it before results
  are certified, not months later.
- The reviews that damaged trust elsewhere handed ballots and machines to private firms. That broke
  the chain of custody, put certified equipment at risk, and still settled nothing.
- Texas already produces most of the records needed: reconciliation forms, manual counts,
  risk-limiting audits, hash tests, device logs, and video. Few counties cross-check them all, and
  fewer publish them. Only 1 of 254 counties posts EMS audit logs online.

## How it works

1. **Request.** Either party's county chair asks, 60 days before the election.
2. **Two co-judges.** Each party's chair appoints one. They are sworn, bonded, and trained. The
   co-judge from the party that carried the county for governor presides, the same rule Texas
   already uses for presiding judges (§32.002) and the Early Voting Ballot Board (§87.002).
3. **Reconcile every loop.** Voters checked in against ballots cast. Mail ballots issued against
   returned, accepted, and counted. Machine totals against ballot images, the manual count, and the
   risk-limiting audit. Ballots printed against issued, spoiled, and unused. Seals and transfer logs
   from start to finish.
4. **Check a sample of equipment.** All election management systems and central scanners, plus a
   random draw of precinct scanners and ballot-marking devices. Each is checked against the
   state-approved software build, its logs are reviewed, its USB media are imaged, and its network
   connections are confirmed off. Each co-judge may add two units. Machines are opened only on a
   real red flag and with Secretary of State approval.
5. **Sign and publish.** Precinct reports, a master discrepancy log, and an assurance statement go
   to the commissioners court with the canvass and are posted online. Passwords and exploitable
   details are redacted.

## Why it cannot stall certification

| If… | then… |
|---|---|
| a count does not match | redo it together until two passes agree (as Texas hand counts already do) |
| the co-judges disagree on why | the presiding co-judge decides in writing, and the disputed items are sealed and saved |
| a co-judge still disagrees | the dissent is recorded, but it is not a veto |
| something is unresolved | only that item goes to the commissioners court, then to a recount or contest |
| a party boycotts | the work continues under two-person control, on video |
| time runs out | the examination closes 2 days before the canvass deadline, and the canvass happens on time |

## Talking points by audience

**Legislators**
- It extends tools Texas already trusts: presiding and alternate judges, the Early Voting Ballot
  Board and Signature Verification Committee, hand-count reconciliation, and recount procedures.
  It is not a new "audit agency."
- It is a single subchapter in Chapter 127, next to the manual count and the risk-limiting audit.
- It keeps the canvass inside the current 11- or 14-day window. No deadline moves.
- The estimated state cost is about $1 million to $1.4 million a year, mostly for a state examiner
  team that counties can use for free.

**County party chairs**
- It gives you a statutory right to request an examination, not a favor you have to ask for.
- Your co-judge sees every step, asks questions on the record, and can flag two machines of your
  choosing.
- Your dissent becomes part of the official record and can be used in a recount or contest.

**Election administrators**
- You keep custody and control. Examiners work as your agents, on your hardware, in your building.
- Non-invasive checks use the system's own export functions and do not touch certification.
- A missing co-judge or a refused signature cannot hold you hostage. Your signature completes
  every report.
- Machine-readable forms from the Secretary of State and an exemption for read-only analysis
  software make the work practical within the canvass window.

## What it does not do

- It does not change any vote total. Corrections happen only through existing law.
- It does not let any party, candidate, or private firm take ballots, machines, or data.
- It does not reopen ballot-acceptance decisions made by the Early Voting Ballot Board or the
  Signature Verification Committee.
- It does not claim to prove a machine was never hacked. Every report says what the check can and
  cannot show. The paper ballots remain the record of the vote.

## Key dates

The act takes effect **September 1, 2027**. The Secretary of State's rules are due by
**December 1, 2027**. The first elections covered are those held on or after **January 1, 2028**,
and the November 2028 general election is the first major one.
