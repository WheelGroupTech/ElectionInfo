# Texas Early Voting Rosters: County and State Data Review

**Election:** November 3, 2026 General Election\
**Data through:** Thursday, October 29, 2026 (eleventh day of in-person early voting)\
**Prepared:** October 30, 2026 (updates the reviews of October 20-29; county files as posted by 12:30 PM October 30, with Wichita's latest files downloaded by hand that morning; state files as posted on the evening of October 30)\
**Scope:** the 50 most populous Texas counties and the Texas Secretary of State (SOS) roster portal

## 1. Summary

Texas Election Code §87.121 requires each county to post its early voting rosters (in-person and by mail) daily and to submit them for statewide elections to the Secretary of State, which republishes them. This update compares the county and state lists voter by voter (by Voter Unique Identifier, VUID) for the first eleven days of in-person voting, October 19-29, in the 50 most populous counties.

Key findings:

1. **The two sources agree closely, but not completely, in every county.** Over every day for which both are complete, the county's and the state's lists of early in-person voters agree on 99.0% or more of voters in 32 of the 33 counties with dated county rosters, and on 98.6% in Guadalupe. In none of them do the two lists match exactly: every county has at least one voter on one list and not the other. (In this review a 100% figure is shown only when no voter is on one list and not the other.) Fourteen voters are an in-person voter in one source and a mail voter in the other (Hunt 11, and one each in Collin, Hidalgo and Cameron).
2. **October 29 is in for every county, and only Harris's file is partial.** All 50 counties have a state in-person file for each of October 19-29. The state has filled in Harris's October 28 file (78,677 of the county's 78,747 voters). For October 29, Harris's file holds 61,712 of the county's 88,984 voters; every other county's holds at least 98.8% of the county's count.
3. **The state keeps revising earlier days, and Brazos's mail ballots now reach it.** Since the evening of October 29, 242 county-day counts for October 14-28 changed in the state's files. 169 are mail counts (net +1,107 voters, 74 of them downward). The 73 in-person counts are net +46,961, almost all of it Harris's October 28 file filled in (+46,599); the other 72 net +362. Brazos, which had 9 mail voters at the state, now has 939, filed back to October 14. The state again moved mail ballots between days: Lubbock's October 23 mail rose from 197 to 378 while its October 15 fell from 101 to 43; Rockwall's October 21 rose from 8 to 106; Wichita's October 23 fell from 33 to 1 and its October 16 from 45 to 12. The October 29 files filled in during the day: between the afternoon and evening downloads Collin's grew from 24 to 29,812 records, Fort Bend's from 1,189 to 21,511 and Harris's from 8,158 to 62,981.
4. **Seventeen voters are recorded as voting in two counties.** New since the last review: four voters who voted in person on October 29 in Brazoria, El Paso, Williamson and Dallas, having voted in person in Harris (October 22), Randall (October 22), Harris (October 24) and Bexar (October 21). Three have the same first and last name in both counties; the Bexar/Dallas voter has the same first name and a different last name. The state lists each only once: in the first county's file, except that it files the Harris vote of October 24 in its Williamson file, with the Harris precinct (§6.8).
5. **Six more voters have both a mail ballot and an in-person vote:** two in Dallas and one in Grayson who voted in person on October 29, with mail ballots the county lists as received October 24 (Dallas) and October 14 (Grayson); two in Jefferson, on its mail list through October 29, who voted in person October 29; and one in Travis who voted in person October 28 and whose mail ballot Travis lists as received October 29. The state lists only the in-person vote for all six. One Bastrop voter listed both ways a day earlier is no longer on Bastrop's mail list. Thirty-four voters are now listed both ways in the same county (§6.9).
6. **The turnout table still overstates Travis, Ellis, Hays, Coryell and Henderson, and now Orange, Potter and Smith for October 29.** For October 23 the portal's table shows 50,268 Travis in-person voters, against 40,484 in the state's own Travis file and 40,453 in Travis County's roster.
7. **Mail lists that trail the state's.** Collin's mail list posted October 30 runs through October 28 and lacks 925 state mail voters (714 dated October 29 and 172 October 28 by the state). Also behind: Cameron 357 (latest list through October 27), Galveston 325 (218 dated October 29; 91 dated October 27, though its list runs through October 28), Wichita 119 (72 dated October 29) and Tom Green 64 (48 dated October 29). El Paso's and Gregg's lists have caught up (16 and 80 state voters missing, down from 193 and 140).
8. **New file formats and names.** Bexar renamed its daily files on October 29: the in-person list's date is written "2026 - 10 - 29", which Election Explorer's loader did not recognize until an update on October 30 (all 40,159 records now dated; 40,124 of the 40,158 county and 40,156 state voters on both), and the mail list is named for 2023 ("2023-10-29") though it holds October 29, 2026. Dallas renamed its in-person file again ("In-Person-Early-Voting-Report_10.29.26.csv"), now without the polling-site and county ID columns. Travis's ZIP of October 30 holds a second correction of its October 5 mail list ("_UPDATED_2") beside the first, and a correction of its October 28 mail list named "(UPDATE)"; the loader now uses only the latest correction of each list. Angelina's October 29 history file, printed two pages wide without column titles, is read after a loader update (1,363 voters, all on the state's file).
9. **Webb's October 28 mail list, which gives county certificate numbers instead of VUIDs, was matched by name:** 47 of its 52 voters match one of the state's 47 Webb mail voters of October 28 (44 by full name, 3 with the first name cut short in Webb's PDF); the other 5 have no state mail voter of that name. Webb's October 29 list gives VUIDs again.
10. **Williamson's mail precincts are still wrong.** Its October 29 file gives a precinct different from the state's for 4,685 mail voters (and three in-person voters).
11. **Bowie's October 20, 21 and 23 reports disagree with the state** on the precinct of 4,718 voters; its other reports match. **Brazos's mail precincts** differ from the state's for 163 mail voters (141 of them in its October 19-20 mail list), now that its mail ballots reach the state.
12. **The state's county files contain voters of other counties.** Across October 14-29, 2,500 voters (2,467 in-person, 33 mail) appear in an SOS county file while being on a different county's own roster, with that county's precinct.
13. **The state's files continue to name voters that Tarrant County redacts.** Tarrant's cumulative in-person list held 51 redacted records on October 30 (the list through October 29), down from 53; its cumulative mail list held 169, down from 175.
14. **Records that warrant review by the counties** (none is proof of wrongdoing; most have clerical explanations):
    - 34 voters listed both as a returned mail ballot and as an early in-person voter in the same county (Dallas 15, El Paso 7, Grayson 3, Jefferson 3, Tarrant 2, Tom Green 2, Travis 2);
    - 59 VUIDs that name entirely different people in two records, 10 of them on Bell's roster;
    - 190 same-day duplicate check-ins in 12 counties (154 of them in Angelina), and 31 mail ballots listed as received on two days (15 in Bexar);
    - precinct differences besides Williamson, Bowie and Brazos: Harris 2,619, Tarrant 268, El Paso 210, Galveston 185, Smith 94, Bexar 70.

## 2. Scope and method

**Counties (by 2025 Census population):**

- Ranks 1-20: Harris, Dallas, Tarrant, Bexar, Travis, Collin, Denton, Fort Bend, Hidalgo, El Paso, Montgomery, Williamson, Cameron, Brazoria, Bell, Galveston, Nueces, Lubbock, Hays, Webb.
- Ranks 21-50: McLennan, Jefferson, Smith, Brazos, Ellis, Johnson, Kaufman, Comal, Guadalupe, Midland, Parker, Ector, Grayson, Randall, Taylor, Rockwall, Wichita, Gregg, Hunt, Liberty, Tom Green, Bastrop, Potter, Bowie, Victoria, Henderson, Angelina, Orange, Coryell, Walker.

**Sources:**

- each county's own roster files, downloaded daily October 20-30. 34 counties post their own rosters, and all 34 are compared in the tables below (Guadalupe's mail lists are scanned images, so only its in-person rosters are compared). The other 16 (Nueces, Lubbock, Hays, Ellis, Johnson, Kaufman, Comal, Midland, Parker, Ector, Taylor, Rockwall, Liberty, Orange, Coryell, Walker) link to the SOS portal;
- the SOS portal's per-county early voting CSVs for October 14-29, for all 50 counties, all days re-downloaded on the evening of October 30, since the state revises them (a second complete pass found no further change);
- the SOS turnout tables for October 19-29.

**Method.** All files were read with Election Explorer's roster loader, which normalizes each county's layout to VUID, precinct, name, voting method and date. For every county file, the number of VUIDs loaded matches an independent count of the 10-digit identifiers in the raw file exactly for 436 of the 441 files read. Of the other five: Dallas's in-person CSV and El Paso's October 29 file each hold one 10-digit number in an address, not a voter; one Travis mail workbook differs by one identifier, as in earlier reviews; in Guadalupe's October 26 roster the loader finds 14 VUIDs the independent count misses (the PDF prints them run together with the ZIP code); and in its October 27 roster, the loader keeps 20 rows without a VUID, and the independent count finds 12 more VUIDs than the loader reads. Two loader updates were made for this review: Bexar's October 29 in-person file name ("2026 - 10 - 29") is now read as a date, and when Travis posts a list and two corrections of it, only the latest correction is read (the October 29 review counted 122 rows of Travis's October 5 mail list twice; its counts of distinct VUIDs were not affected). Webb's October 28 mail list gives no VUIDs; its voters were matched by name to the state's Webb mail voters (exact last and first name, or the same last name with one first name the start of the other), which assigned VUIDs to 47 of its 52 rows. One correction was made before comparing: Tom Green's latest roster (posted October 29, named "102826") holds 330 records of October 29, part of that day, which are left out. Angelina's lists were taken from its cumulative October 25 file and its October 26-29 files. The lists were then compared by VUID:

- *early in-person, by day*: the county's in-person voters for each of October 19-29 vs. the SOS's IN-PERSON voters for that date. For counties whose lists are cumulative and undated (Tarrant, Hidalgo, Denton, El Paso, Potter), each day was recovered from consecutive daily snapshots (a day's voters = that day's snapshot minus the previous day's). A day counts toward the totals only when both sources have it and the state's file holds at least 90% of the county's count;
- *mail*: the county's latest mail list (or all daily files) vs. the SOS's MAIL-IN voters for October 14-29.

"County-only" and "state-only" voters were checked against the other 49 counties' files. Name and precinct comparisons ignore formatting (case, spacing, leading zeros, "Precinct"/"P"/"S" prefixes, and a two-digit precinct split such as "418603" for precinct 4186). Voter names and VUIDs are deliberately not reproduced in this report; the companion supplement lists the VUIDs and file locations of the anomalies for county and state staff.

## 3. The Secretary of State portal

**Where:** "Election Information & Turnout Data", `https://goelect.txelections.civixapps.com/ivis-evr-ui/evr`. Pick an election and an early voting date, then *Submit*: a table of registered voters, in-person and mail turnout per county, with a **CSV Download** per county under *Voter Details Report*.

**How the data is served.** The page fetches JSON whose `upload` field is the file in base64:

| Data | Request |
|---|---|
| Elections, early voting dates and county ids | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFile?type=EVR_ELECTION` |
| Turnout table for one date | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFile?type=EVR_EARLYVOTING&electionId=53815&electionDate=10/29/2026` |
| One county's voter CSV for one date | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFileByFormat?type=EVR_EARLYVOTING&electionId=53815&countyId=<id>&county=<NAME>&electionDate=<MM/DD/YYYY>&format=CSV` |

County ids follow alphabetical order, except that the portal sorts McLennan after McCulloch (id 161). An empty response means the county reported nothing for that day.

**File contents.** `VOTER_NAME` ("LAST, FIRST MIDDLE"), `ID_VOTER` (VUID), `VOTING_METHOD` (`IN-PERSON` / `MAIL-IN`), `PRECINCT`. There is **no address and no date column**. A county CSV holds only the voters credited on that date.

**Timeliness and revisions.** A day's files fill in over the following day or two. By the evening of October 30, all 50 counties had an in-person file for each of October 19-29. Harris's October 28 file, partial a day earlier, is now complete (78,677 of the county's 78,747), and for October 29 only Harris's is partial (61,712 of 88,984); the only other partial files are Gregg's October 19 and Hunt's October 20, partial since the first week. Nueces's October 29 file holds 6,344 voters; its October 25 and 27 files remain small (534 and 1,889). Re-downloading every day for every county on the evening of October 30 changed 251 files for October 14-29 from the afternoon download. Compared with the files used in the October 29 review, 242 county-day counts for October 14-28 differ: 169 mail counts (net +1,107 voters, 74 of them downward) and 73 in-person counts (net +46,961, of which Harris's October 28 file +46,599; Williamson's October 28 +57 and Bexar's +52; 22 downward). Brazos's mail ballots, almost absent until now, are filed back to October 14 (186 for October 19, 142 for October 26, 112 each for October 21 and 22; 939 in all). The state re-dates mail ballots as well as adding them: Lubbock's October 23 mail rose from 197 to 378 while its October 15 fell from 101 to 43; Rockwall's October 21 rose from 8 to 106 and Johnson's October 26 from 114 to 206; Wichita's October 27 rose from 243 to 294 and its October 21 from 261 to 300, while its October 19 fell from 313 to 266, its October 16 from 45 to 12 and its October 23 from 33 to 1; Jefferson's October 27 fell from 50 to 8 and Cameron's October 21 from 222 to 161. The October 29 files themselves grew between the afternoon and evening downloads of October 30: Collin from 24 to 29,812 records, Fort Bend from 1,189 to 21,511 and Harris from 8,158 to 62,981.

**Mail dates.** The date of an SOS mail file is not the day the county received the ballot, and the state moves ballots from one day's file to another. In Travis, the state's October 16 mail voters are almost all ballots the county lists as received October 13, and its October 19 mail voters mostly the county's October 14 ballots. Mail comparisons in this review are therefore made over all days together.

**Turnout table vs. CSVs.** The statewide table now shows 784,598 in-person voters for October 19, 745,499 for October 20, 684,247 for October 21, 633,713 for October 22, 701,210 for October 23, 518,765 for October 24, 268,434 for October 25, 584,110 for October 26, 551,196 for October 27, 549,444 for October 28 and 561,989 for October 29. It agrees with the state's CSVs for every county and day except Travis (October 23: table 50,268, CSV 40,484), Ellis (October 23: 8,108 vs. 6,326), Hays (October 22: 10,861 vs. 8,791), Coryell (October 20: 1,867 vs. 1,442), Henderson (October 25: 513 vs. 441) and, for October 29, Orange (1,602 vs. 1,288), Potter (1,912 vs. 1,560) and Smith (5,130 vs. 4,960); Potter's and Smith's own rosters show 1,556 and 4,994 for that day.

**Name formatting.** The SOS appends name suffixes to the middle name without a space ("RAYJR"), which causes apparent name mismatches against county files.

## 6. Anomalies between county and state data

### 6.1 State county files include voters of other counties

Across October 14-29, 2,500 voters (2,467 in-person, 33 mail) in the SOS county files appear on a different county's own roster, with the same first name and that county's precinct. The largest numbers are in the SOS files for Travis (254), Dallas (243), Denton (214), Bexar (207), Collin (136), Johnson (114), Hays (110), Harris (103), Tarrant (103) and Parker (86). No voter appears in two SOS county files, so these are records assigned to the wrong county's file, not duplicates. Anyone using SOS county files alone will slightly overcount each county.

### 6.2 State files identify voters Tarrant County redacts

Tarrant replaces the name, VUID and address of protected voters with "--Redacted--", keeping only precinct and date: 220 records in its current files (169 mail, 51 early in-person). No other county redacts identities; Harris hides the address (only) of named voters.

- **Mail:** the SOS Tarrant mail files list 150 mail voters who are not on Tarrant's mail roster (149 after excluding one voter of another county). For 120 of them there is a redacted Tarrant mail record with the same precinct and the same date.
- **In person:** the SOS Tarrant files list 92 in-person voters (besides 102 of other counties) who are not on Tarrant's roster on any day; 50 of them match a redacted Tarrant in-person record by precinct.
- **Redacted counts:** Tarrant's cumulative in-person list held 104 redacted records on October 20, falling each day to 47 in the afternoon file of October 27, then 53 in the evening file of October 27 and on October 29, and 51 on October 30 (the list through October 29); its cumulative mail list held 215 in the October 23 afternoon file, 202 in the evening file, 192 on October 25, 186 on October 27, 182 in the October 27 evening file, 175 on October 29 and 169 on October 30. A cumulative list should not shrink.

With eleven days of data, the match remains close for both voting methods. This continues to indicate that the state publishes the name, VUID and precinct of voters whose identities Tarrant withholds. It cannot be proven record by record, because the redacted rows carry no identifier. Neither source exposes an address.

### 6.3 Reporting gaps in the state data

- **In-person:** every county has a state file for every day of October 19-29; Harris's October 29 file is partial (61,712 of 88,984). Two earlier days remain partial: Gregg's October 19 (3 of 3,677) and Hunt's October 20 (1,724 of 2,044). Nueces's October 25 and 27 files are small (534 and 1,889 voters).

## 8. Recommendations

1. **Download daily** through Election Day: Tarrant, Hidalgo, Denton, El Paso and Potter (no voting date per voter), Victoria, Tom Green, Jefferson and McLennan (cumulative files replaced), and Collin, Williamson, Cameron, Dallas, Fort Bend and Harris (latest file only). 6. **Repeat this comparison** after early voting closes and after the canvass.

## Appendix A. Files

Downloaded files are saved per county:

- county files are saved as `G26_<county file name>`; files a county posts under the same name every day get the download date (`_2026-10-30`), or the download date and time (`_2026-10-30-12-31-13`) for cumulative lists that carry no voting dates (Hidalgo, Potter);
- SOS files are saved as `G26_<COUNTY>.2026 NOVEMBER GENERAL ELECTION.EarlyVoting.<MM_DD_YYYY>.csv`; when the state revises a day, the earlier copy is kept.

The files load in Election Explorer (File → Load Voter Roster); the comparisons in this report were produced from the loader's normalized output. The companion supplement (`Texas_Early_Voting_Roster_Review_2026-10-30_Supplement`) lists each anomalous record's VUID and its location in the county's and the state's files.

## Appendix B. Caveats

- In-person comparisons cover October 19-29; where the state's file for a day is partial or missing, that day is shown but left out of the totals.
- County files were downloaded October 20-30 at different times; several counties post same-day lists that are later replaced, and counties keep revising earlier days. The state files were re-downloaded about nine hours after the county files on October 30.
- Day-level counts for cumulative, undated county lists were recovered from consecutive snapshots and attribute voters added late to the day they first appear (Tarrant, §5.1).
- A match between two records means the same VUID; name and precinct differences were checked separately. A match by name, precinct or date without a common VUID (§2, §6.2, §6.11) is an inference, not a confirmed identity.
- This review describes data as published. It does not establish that any voter voted improperly; the records flagged in §6.5-§6.10 have ordinary clerical explanations as well as serious ones, and only the counties can resolve them.
