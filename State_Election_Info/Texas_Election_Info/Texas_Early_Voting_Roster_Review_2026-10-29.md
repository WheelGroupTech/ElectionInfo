# Texas Early Voting Rosters: County and State Data Review

**Election:** November 3, 2026 General Election\
**Data through:** Wednesday, October 28, 2026 (tenth day of in-person early voting)\
**Prepared:** October 29, 2026 (updates the reviews of October 20-28; county files as posted by 2:00 PM October 29, with Wichita's latest files downloaded by hand that afternoon; state files as posted by 8:00 PM October 29)\
**Scope:** the 50 most populous Texas counties and the Texas Secretary of State (SOS) roster portal

## 1. Summary

Texas Election Code §87.121 requires each county to post its early voting rosters (in-person and by mail) daily and to submit them for statewide elections to the Secretary of State, which republishes them. This update compares the county and state lists voter by voter (by Voter Unique Identifier, VUID) for the first ten days of in-person voting, October 19-28, in the 50 most populous counties.

Key findings:

1. **The two sources agree closely, but not completely, in every county.** Over every day for which both are complete, the county's and the state's lists of early in-person voters agree on 99.0% or more of voters in 32 of the 33 counties with dated county rosters, and on 98.6% in Guadalupe. In none of them do the two lists match exactly: every county has at least one voter on one list and not the other. (In this review a 100% figure is shown only when no voter is on one list and not the other.) Eleven voters are an in-person voter in one source and a mail voter in the other (Hunt 5, Collin 4, and one each in Hidalgo and Cameron).
2. **October 28 is in for every county, and only Harris's file is partial.** All 50 counties have a state in-person file for each of October 19-28. The state has filled in Harris's October 27 file (75,185 of the county's 75,247 voters). For October 28, Harris's file holds 32,078 of the county's 78,747 voters; every other county's holds at least 97% of the county's count.
3. **The state keeps revising earlier days.** Since the evening of October 28, 181 county-day counts for October 14-27 changed in the state's files. 125 are mail counts (net +793 voters, 63 of them downward). The 56 in-person counts are net +16,862, almost all of it Harris's October 27 file filled in (+16,327); the other 55 net +535. The state again moved mail ballots between days: Brazoria's October 26 mail rose from 382 to 649 and its October 20 from 1,651 to 1,867, while its October 21-24 fell to about a third (from 88, 117, 77 and 74 to 31, 39, 24 and 21); Williamson's October 23 fell from 125 to 36 and Lubbock's October 15 from 177 to 101. The October 28 files filled in during the day: between the afternoon and evening downloads Collin's grew from 3,447 to 27,619 records, Denton's from 13 to 21,839, Fort Bend's from 1,877 to 18,792 and Harris's from 4,814 to 33,754.
4. **Thirteen voters are recorded as voting in two counties.** New since the last review: two voters on Tarrant's cumulative in-person list from October 28 who are on Bexar's roster for October 20 and on El Paso's list from October 25 (same names), and one voter on Hidalgo's list from October 28 who is on Bexar's roster for October 25 (same first name, different last name). The state lists each of the three only in the first county's file (Bexar, El Paso, Bexar). The other ten are unchanged (§6.8).
5. **Seven more voters have both a mail ballot and an in-person vote:** five in Dallas who voted in person on October 28, with mail ballots the county lists as received October 24 (3) or October 25 (2), and two in Tom Green who voted in person on October 27 (mail ballots received September 30 and October 5), found now that Tom Green's roster holds all of October 27. The state lists only the in-person vote for six of the seven. Twenty-nine voters are now listed both ways in the same county (§6.9).
6. **The turnout table still overstates Travis, Ellis, Hays, Coryell and Henderson.** For October 23 the portal's table shows 50,268 Travis in-person voters, against 40,484 in the state's own Travis file and 40,453 in Travis County's roster.
7. **Collin's and Victoria's mail lists have caught up.** Collin's October 28 mail list lacks 23 of the state's Collin mail voters (291 a day earlier), and Victoria's, whose mail ballots stopped at October 23 until now, lacks 11. Lists that trail the state's: Cameron 210 (97 dated October 28 and 83 October 27 by the state; Cameron's latest list runs through October 27), El Paso 193 (176 dated October 28; latest list through October 27), Gregg 140 (latest list October 24-25), Galveston 103 (91 dated October 27, though its list runs through October 28) and Wichita 78 (72 dated October 28).
8. **New file formats.** Dallas now posts its in-person details as a CSV with no date in its name (488,019 records, read in full). Webb's October 28 mail list gives a county "Certificate" number in place of the VUID, so it cannot be matched to the state's records; Webb's mail is compared through its October 27 list. Guadalupe's October 27 roster and the page of Gregg's October 27 list that the last review could not read are now read in full, after updates to Election Explorer's loader.
9. **Bexar's October 28 check-in list is again named for 2028** ("10282028"). It is read as October 28, 2026, and agrees with the state's file on 41,138 county and 41,217 state voters.
10. **Williamson's mail precincts are still wrong.** Its October 28 file gives a precinct different from the state's for 4,685 mail voters (and one in-person voter).
11. **Bowie's October 20, 21 and 23 reports disagree with the state** on the precinct of 4,718 voters; its other reports match.
12. **The state's county files contain voters of other counties.** Across October 14-28, 2,236 voters (2,200 in-person, 36 mail) appear in an SOS county file while being on a different county's own roster, with that county's precinct.
13. **The state's files continue to name voters that Tarrant County redacts.** Tarrant's cumulative in-person list held 53 redacted records on October 29 (the list through October 28), as on the evening of October 27; its cumulative mail list held 175, down from 182.
14. **Records that warrant review by the counties** (none is proof of wrongdoing; most have clerical explanations):
    - 29 voters listed both as a returned mail ballot and as an early in-person voter in the same county (Dallas 13, El Paso 7, Grayson 2, Tarrant 2, Tom Green 2, Bastrop, Jefferson and Travis 1 each);
    - 56 VUIDs that name entirely different people in two records, 10 of them on Bell's roster;
    - 183 same-day duplicate check-ins in 11 counties (149 of them in Angelina), and 28 mail ballots listed as received on two days (11 in Bexar);
    - precinct differences besides Williamson and Bowie: Harris 1,787, Tarrant 237, Galveston 178, El Paso 172, Smith 84, Bexar 69.

## 2. Scope and method

**Counties (by 2025 Census population):**

- Ranks 1-20: Harris, Dallas, Tarrant, Bexar, Travis, Collin, Denton, Fort Bend, Hidalgo, El Paso, Montgomery, Williamson, Cameron, Brazoria, Bell, Galveston, Nueces, Lubbock, Hays, Webb.
- Ranks 21-50: McLennan, Jefferson, Smith, Brazos, Ellis, Johnson, Kaufman, Comal, Guadalupe, Midland, Parker, Ector, Grayson, Randall, Taylor, Rockwall, Wichita, Gregg, Hunt, Liberty, Tom Green, Bastrop, Potter, Bowie, Victoria, Henderson, Angelina, Orange, Coryell, Walker.

**Sources:**

- each county's own roster files, downloaded daily October 20-29. 34 counties post their own rosters, and all 34 are compared in the tables below (Guadalupe's mail lists are scanned images, so only its in-person rosters are compared). The other 16 (Nueces, Lubbock, Hays, Ellis, Johnson, Kaufman, Comal, Midland, Parker, Ector, Taylor, Rockwall, Liberty, Orange, Coryell, Walker) link to the SOS portal;
- the SOS portal's per-county early voting CSVs for October 14-28, for all 50 counties, all days re-downloaded on the evening of October 29, since the state revises them (a second complete pass found no further change);
- the SOS turnout tables for October 19-28.

**Method.** All files were read with Election Explorer's roster loader, which normalizes each county's layout to VUID, precinct, name, voting method and date. For every county file, the number of VUIDs loaded matches an independent count of the 10-digit identifiers in the raw file exactly for 408 of the 413 files read. Of the other five: Dallas's new CSV and El Paso's October 28 file each hold one 10-digit number in an address, not a voter; one Travis mail workbook differs by one identifier, as in earlier reviews; in Guadalupe's October 26 roster the loader finds 14 VUIDs the independent count misses (the PDF prints them run together with the ZIP code); and in its October 27 roster, the loader keeps 20 rows without a VUID, and the independent count finds 12 more VUIDs than the loader reads. Webb's October 28 mail list, which has no VUIDs, is not compared. One correction was made before comparing: Tom Green's roster posted October 29 (named "102826") holds 330 voters of October 29, part of that day, which are left out. Angelina's lists were taken from its cumulative October 25 file and its October 26, 27 and 28 files. The lists were then compared by VUID:

- *early in-person, by day*: the county's in-person voters for each of October 19-28 vs. the SOS's IN-PERSON voters for that date. For counties whose lists are cumulative and undated (Tarrant, Hidalgo, Denton, El Paso, Potter), each day was recovered from consecutive daily snapshots (a day's voters = that day's snapshot minus the previous day's). A day counts toward the totals only when both sources have it and the state's file holds at least 90% of the county's count;
- *mail*: the county's latest mail list (or all daily files) vs. the SOS's MAIL-IN voters for October 14-28.

"County-only" and "state-only" voters were checked against the other 49 counties' files. Name and precinct comparisons ignore formatting (case, spacing, leading zeros, "Precinct"/"P"/"S" prefixes, and a two-digit precinct split such as "418603" for precinct 4186). Voter names and VUIDs are deliberately not reproduced in this report; the companion supplement lists the VUIDs and file locations of the anomalies for county and state staff.

## 3. The Secretary of State portal

**Where:** "Election Information & Turnout Data", `https://goelect.txelections.civixapps.com/ivis-evr-ui/evr`. Pick an election and an early voting date, then *Submit*: a table of registered voters, in-person and mail turnout per county, with a **CSV Download** per county under *Voter Details Report*.

**How the data is served.** The page fetches JSON whose `upload` field is the file in base64:

| Data | Request |
|---|---|
| Elections, early voting dates and county ids | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFile?type=EVR_ELECTION` |
| Turnout table for one date | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFile?type=EVR_EARLYVOTING&electionId=53815&electionDate=10/28/2026` |
| One county's voter CSV for one date | `/api-ivis-system/api/v1/getFileByFormat?type=EVR_EARLYVOTING&electionId=53815&countyId=<id>&county=<NAME>&electionDate=<MM/DD/YYYY>&format=CSV` |

County ids follow alphabetical order, except that the portal sorts McLennan after McCulloch (id 161). An empty response means the county reported nothing for that day.

**File contents.** `VOTER_NAME` ("LAST, FIRST MIDDLE"), `ID_VOTER` (VUID), `VOTING_METHOD` (`IN-PERSON` / `MAIL-IN`), `PRECINCT`. There is **no address and no date column**. A county CSV holds only the voters credited on that date.

**Timeliness and revisions.** A day's files fill in over the following day or two. By the evening of October 29, all 50 counties had an in-person file for each of October 19-28. Harris's October 27 file, partial a day earlier, is now complete (75,185 of the county's 75,247), and for October 28 only Harris's is partial (32,078 of 78,747); the only other partial files are Gregg's October 19 and Hunt's October 20, partial since the first week. Nueces's October 28 file holds 5,954 voters; its October 25 and 27 files remain small (534 and 1,889). Re-downloading every day for every county on the evening of October 29 changed 225 files for October 14-28 from the afternoon download. Compared with the files used in the October 28 review, 181 county-day counts for October 14-27 differ: 125 mail counts (net +793 voters, 63 of them downward) and 56 in-person counts (net +16,862, of which Harris's October 27 file +16,327; Dallas's October 19 +207; 15 downward). The state re-dates mail ballots as well as adding them: Brazoria's October 26 mail rose from 382 to 649 and its October 20 from 1,651 to 1,867, while its October 21, 22, 23 and 24 fell from 88, 117, 77 and 74 to 31, 39, 24 and 21; Williamson's October 23 fell from 125 to 36 and its October 24 from 976 to 901, while its October 27 rose from 770 to 937; Lubbock's October 20 rose from 354 to 523 while its October 14 and 15 fell from 136 and 177 to 90 and 101; Wichita's October 22 rose from 39 to 119 while its October 19 fell from 369 to 313. Dallas (153), McLennan (71) and Rockwall (43) now have mail voters for October 27, which had none a day earlier. The October 28 files themselves grew between the afternoon and evening downloads of October 29: Collin from 3,447 to 27,619 records, Denton from 13 to 21,839, Fort Bend from 1,877 to 18,792, Harris from 4,814 to 33,754, Bexar from 37,958 to 42,397 and Gregg from 2 to 1,671.

**Mail dates.** The date of an SOS mail file is not the day the county received the ballot, and the state moves ballots from one day's file to another. In Travis, the state's October 16 mail voters are almost all ballots the county lists as received October 13, and its October 19 mail voters mostly the county's October 14 ballots. Mail comparisons in this review are therefore made over all days together.

**Turnout table vs. CSVs.** The statewide table now shows 784,604 in-person voters for October 19, 745,511 for October 20, 684,226 for October 21, 633,693 for October 22, 698,930 for October 23, 518,071 for October 24, 268,405 for October 25, 583,091 for October 26, 551,116 for October 27 and 499,433 for October 28. It agrees with the state's CSVs for every county and day except Travis (October 23: table 50,268, CSV 40,484), Ellis (October 23: 8,108 vs. 6,326), Hays (October 22: 10,861 vs. 8,791), Coryell (October 20: 1,867 vs. 1,442) and Henderson (October 25: 513 vs. 441).

**Name formatting.** The SOS appends name suffixes to the middle name without a space ("RAYJR"), which causes apparent name mismatches against county files.

## 6. Anomalies between county and state data

### 6.1 State county files include voters of other counties

Across October 14-28, 2,236 voters (2,200 in-person, 36 mail) in the SOS county files appear on a different county's own roster, with the same first name and that county's precinct. The largest numbers are in the SOS files for Travis (233), Dallas (215), Denton (201), Bexar (187), Collin (121), Hays (99), Johnson (99), Harris (93), Tarrant (87) and Parker (78). No voter appears in two SOS county files, so these are records assigned to the wrong county's file, not duplicates. Anyone using SOS county files alone will slightly overcount each county.

### 6.2 State files identify voters Tarrant County redacts

Tarrant replaces the name, VUID and address of protected voters with "--Redacted--", keeping only precinct and date: 228 records in its current files (175 mail, 53 early in-person). No other county redacts identities; Harris hides the address (only) of 3,156 named voters.

- **Mail:** the SOS Tarrant mail files list 145 mail voters who are not on Tarrant's mail roster (144 after excluding one voter of another county). For 122 of them there is a redacted Tarrant mail record with the same precinct and the same date.
- **In person:** the SOS Tarrant files list 92 in-person voters (besides 86 of other counties) who are not on Tarrant's roster on any day; 52 of them match a redacted Tarrant in-person record by precinct.
- **Redacted counts:** Tarrant's cumulative in-person list held 104 redacted records on October 20, falling each day to 47 in the afternoon file of October 27, then 53 in the evening file of October 27 and again on October 29 (the list through October 28); its cumulative mail list held 215 in the October 23 afternoon file, 202 in the evening file, 192 on October 25, 186 on October 27, 182 in the October 27 evening file and 175 on October 29. A cumulative list should not shrink.

With ten days of data, the match remains close for both voting methods. This continues to indicate that the state publishes the name, VUID and precinct of voters whose identities Tarrant withholds. It cannot be proven record by record, because the redacted rows carry no identifier. Neither source exposes an address.

### 6.3 Reporting gaps in the state data

- **In-person:** every county has a state file for every day of October 19-28; Harris's October 28 file is partial (32,078 of 78,747). Two earlier days remain partial: Gregg's October 19 (3 of 3,677) and Hunt's October 20 (1,724 of 2,044). Nueces's October 25 and 27 files are small (534 and 1,889 voters).
- **Mail:** Brazos (9 of 1,768) and Ector (2) report almost none to the state.
- **Turnout table vs. CSV:** Travis, Ellis, Hays, Coryell and Henderson (§3).

## Appendix A. Files

Downloaded files are saved per county:

- county files are saved as `G26_<county file name>`; files a county posts under the same name every day get the download date (`_2026-10-29`), or the download date and time (`_2026-10-29-13-36-49`) for cumulative lists that carry no voting dates (Hidalgo, Potter);
- SOS files are saved as `G26_<COUNTY>.2026 NOVEMBER GENERAL ELECTION.EarlyVoting.<MM_DD_YYYY>.csv`; when the state revises a day, the earlier copy is kept.

The files load in Election Explorer (File → Load Voter Roster); the comparisons in this report were produced from the loader's normalized output. The companion supplement (`Texas_Early_Voting_Roster_Review_2026-10-29_Supplement`) lists each anomalous record's VUID and its location in the county's and the state's files.

## Appendix B. Caveats

- In-person comparisons cover October 19-28; where the state's file for a day is partial or missing, that day is shown but left out of the totals.
- County files were downloaded October 20-29 at different times; several counties post same-day lists that are later replaced, and counties keep revising earlier days. The state files were re-downloaded about six hours after the county files on October 29.
- Day-level counts for cumulative, undated county lists were recovered from consecutive snapshots and attribute voters added late to the day they first appear (Tarrant, §5.1).
- A match between two records means the same VUID; name and precinct differences were checked separately. A match by name, precinct or date without a common VUID (§6.2, §6.11) is an inference, not a confirmed identity.
- This review describes data as published. It does not establish that any voter voted improperly; the records flagged in §6.5-§6.10 have ordinary clerical explanations as well as serious ones, and only the counties can resolve them.
